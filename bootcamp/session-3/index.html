Session 3: Getting Clients: Content and Ads

What's covered today

  2. Long-form video: topics, scripts, recording, editing with Claude, YouTube
  3. Short-form video: Claude finds the clips, cuts them and writes the hooks

  6. Action items + Q&A

PART 1 · THE CONTENT ENGINE

One idea becomes a week of content

  • Your buyers don't know you yet. Content is how they meet you before they ever book a call
  • You make one thing a week: a long-form video on one idea. Claude turns it into everything else
  • The same videos become your ad creative in Part 5. You're not making content and ads separately

What one recording turns into

From one videoWhere it goesWho makes it
Long-form videoYouTube, your website, embedded in a blog postYou record. Claude edits
5 to 10 clipsInstagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, FacebookClaude finds and cuts them
Blog postYour websiteThe content writer skill from Session 2
3 to 5 text postsLinkedIn, Facebook groupsClaude, from the transcript
1 emailYour listClaude, from the transcript
Ad creativeFacebook and Instagram adsYour best-performing clips
  • Your weekly time: about 2 hours. One hour to record, one hour to review what Claude made
  • Consistency beats quality. One video a week for a year beats ten great videos and then nothing

Pick topics your buyers actually search and ask

  • Every video answers one question a [niche] business owner asks, in their words
  • Your best source is your own sales calls: the questions prospects ask before they buy
  • Your second source is your content plan from Session 2. A topic that's worth a blog post is worth a video
Claude prompt
Build me a list of 20 video topics for my agency's YouTube channel. My buyers: [niche] business owners. My offer: [services]. Here are the questions prospects ask on sales calls: [paste them]. Pull in the topics from my content plan in Agency OS too. For each topic give me: the video title, the one question it answers, why a buyer would click, and which of my service pages it supports. Lead with problems and losses my buyers feel (lost leads, wasted ad spend, agencies that didn't deliver), not with our services.

PART 2 · LONG-FORM VIDEO

Long-form video: the core of the engine

  • Long-form is where trust gets built. A buyer who watches 15 minutes of you explaining their problem shows up to the call half sold
  • YouTube is also the second biggest search engine, and AI tools cite YouTube videos in their answers
  • Every video follows the same path:

Step 1: Script it with Claude

  • Don't read a script word for word. You'll sound like a robot
  • Have Claude write an outline: the hook, 3 to 5 points, a proof point for each, and the call to action
  • Talk through the outline like you're on a sales call. That's the voice that sells

The outline every video uses

PartWhat it doesLength
HookName the problem and the cost of it in the first 15 seconds15 sec
Why it happensThe mechanism, in plain words1 to 2 min
The fix3 to 5 points, each with a real example or number8 to 12 min
ProofA client result1 min
Next stepOne call to action: the free audit30 sec
Claude prompt
Write me a video outline for the topic: [topic]. My audience is [niche] business owners. Use this structure: a hook that names the problem and what it costs them in the first 15 seconds, why the problem happens, 3 to 5 fixes with a real example or number for each, one client result I can talk about ([result]), and a closing call to action to get a free audit. Give me bullet points I can talk through, not a script to read. Write it the way I talk on sales calls.

Step 2: Record it

  • You don't need a studio. You need clear audio, decent light and a steady shot
  • Audio matters more than video. People will watch a grainy video with good sound. They won't watch the reverse
WhatStarter setupNotes
CameraYour phone or a 1080p webcamEye level, landscape for long-form
MicA clip-on lav or USB micThe biggest upgrade you can buy
LightFace a window, or one LED panelLight in front of you, never behind
Screen recordingScreen Studio, Loom or OBSFor walkthroughs of tools and reports
BackgroundTidy and plainNothing to distract
  • Record in one take. Pause, restart the sentence, keep going. Claude cuts the mistakes out
  • Record your screen and your face at the same time when you're showing a report or a tool

Step 3: Edit it with Claude

  • Claude can edit video in the Claude desktop app. It transcribes the recording, then uses the transcript to cut it
  • It removes the dead air, the false starts and the "um"s, then gives you back a clean file
  • Drop the raw recording into your content folder and run the prompt
Claude prompt
Run this in the Claude desktop app with my content folder connected. Edit my video [file name]. 1. Transcribe it with timestamps. 2. Cut out silences over 1 second, false starts, repeated takes (keep the last one) and filler words. 3. Show me the cut list before you cut anything. 4. Export the clean edit as [title]-edit.mp4 in the same folder, plus the transcript as a text file. Don't change anything else about the video.

Want captions, b-roll or zooms? Ask for them one at a time after the clean cut is done.

Step 4: Publish to YouTube

  • Claude writes everything YouTube needs from the transcript
  • Upload the video yourself in YouTube Studio, then paste in what Claude wrote
  • Title that names the problem, under 60 characters
  • Description with the answer in the first 2 lines, a link to your audit page and a link to the matching service page
  • Chapters with timestamps, so YouTube and Google can show the right moment
  • Thumbnail: your face plus 3 to 4 words. Claude can design it
  • Embed it on the matching blog post or service page on your site
Claude prompt
Here's the transcript of my video: [paste or attach]. Write the YouTube title (under 60 characters, names the problem), the description (answer in the first 2 lines, then a link to [audit page] and [service page]), chapters with timestamps, and 10 tags. Then give me 3 thumbnail text options, 3 to 4 words each.

PART 3 · SHORT-FORM VIDEO

Short-form: Claude finds the clips

  • Inside every 15-minute video are 5 to 10 moments that work on their own: a strong opinion, a number, a quick fix
  • Claude reads the transcript, picks those moments, cuts them vertical and writes the hook and caption for each
  • You review them and post. No CapCut, no editor

What makes a clip work

RuleWhy
The first line is the hookPeople decide in under 2 seconds. Start mid-point, never with "Hey guys"
One idea per clipIf it needs context from earlier in the video, it's not a clip
30 to 60 secondsLong enough to say something, short enough to finish
Captions on screenMost people watch with the sound off
Ends on a point, not a fadeThe last line should make them want the next one
Claude prompt
Run this in the Claude desktop app with my content folder connected. Find the best short clips in [video file] using its transcript [transcript file]. 1. Pick 8 moments, 30 to 60 seconds each, that make sense on their own: a strong opinion, a number, a quick fix, a mistake my buyers make. 2. Start each clip on its strongest line. 3. Show me the list with timestamps and why you picked each one, and wait for my OK. 4. Cut the ones I approve as vertical 9:16 videos with captions burned in, named clip-01.mp4, clip-02.mp4 and so on. 5. For each clip write a hook for the first line on screen, a caption, and 3 hashtags.

PART 4 · DISTRIBUTION AND LEAD CAPTURE

Go where your buyers already are

  • Don't post everywhere. Find the 2 or 3 places your niche actually spends time, and show up there every week
  • For most [niche] business owners that's Facebook groups, LinkedIn and their industry's own events and newsletters
ChannelWhat to postHow to find it
Facebook groupsAnswers, not links. Help first, then mention the auditSearch "[niche] owners" and "[niche] marketing"
LinkedInClips and text posts from your videosConnect with [niche] owners in your area
YouTubeYour long-form videosAlready done in Part 2
Your email listOne email a week from the videoEveryone who takes the audit
Industry spotsGuest posts, podcasts, association newslettersAsk Claude for a list in your niche
Claude prompt
Find where [niche] business owners in [location or country] spend time online. Give me the 10 biggest Facebook groups, LinkedIn groups, subreddits, podcasts and industry newsletters or associations for them, with a link to each, roughly how big it is, and whether it allows people to post. Then tell me which 3 I should focus on first and why.

Your lead magnet: the free audit

  • Every video, clip and post ends with the same offer: a free audit of their website or their marketing
  • It's the most natural next step for a buyer. It shows them their problem with their own numbers
  • You build the audit itself in Session 5 (the Website Quality Audit). Today you set up the page that captures the lead

The audit page

  • Headline that names the problem: "Find out why [niche] businesses lose leads to competitors"
  • Short video of you explaining what the audit shows
  • One button under the text that opens a pop-up form. Never a form sitting on the page
  • Three fields: name, email, website. Every extra field costs you leads
  • Thank-you page with your booking calendar, so the hottest leads book right away
  • Lead goes into Agency OS and gets tagged with where it came from

Build the audit page with Claude

Claude prompt
Run this in the Claude desktop app with my website folder connected. Build a free audit landing page at /audit/ in my site's design. Headline: [headline]. Under it, my video [YouTube link], 3 bullets on what the audit shows, and one button under the text: "Get my free audit". The button opens a pop-up form with name, email and website. On submit, push a form_submit event to the dataLayer, send the lead to Agency OS, and go to /audit/thanks/, which shows my booking calendar [calendar link]. Add it to the board first so I can review it, then publish it.
  • Every piece of content links here. Your YouTube descriptions, your clip captions, your posts, your ads
  • Track it: the form_submit event you set up in Session 2 is how you'll measure which content and which ads bring leads

PART 5 · FACEBOOK ADS

Facebook ads: put money behind what already works

  • Organic content is slow. Ads put your best videos in front of [niche] owners this week
  • You don't make new creative for ads. Your best clips from Part 3 are the ads
  • The goal of the ad is one thing: get the audit. The audit and the call do the selling

Step 1: The offer and the copy

  • Offer: the free audit. Specific, free and about their business
  • Copy is always problem, agitate, solution. Name the problem, make it hurt (lost leads, wasted spend, an agency that didn't deliver), then offer the audit
  • Call out the buyer in the first word: "[Niche] owners..."
  • Lead with the problem in the ad. Let the audit page and the call introduce your solution
Claude prompt
Write Facebook ad copy for my free audit offer. My buyers: [niche] business owners. Their biggest problems: [problems from sales calls]. Use problem, agitate, solution. Start with a callout to the buyer ("[Niche] owners..."). Give me 3 primary text options (short, medium, long), 5 headlines and 3 descriptions. Lead with the problem and what it costs them. Don't name our services or use industry jargon. The call to action is to get the free audit.

Step 2: Set up the campaign

  • Name every ad clearly: "A - [ad name]" for the original, "B - [ad name]" for its test version
  • Keep text clear of the top and bottom of story ads, where the platform buttons sit

Step 3: Turn your clips into ads

  • Pick the 3 to 5 clips people watched to the end
  • Recut each one for ads: the hook in the first 2 seconds, captions on, the audit offer at the end
  • Claude does the recut and makes every size
Claude prompt
Run this in the Claude desktop app with my content folder connected. Turn these clips into Facebook ads: [clip files]. For each one: start on the strongest line, keep it under 45 seconds, burn in captions, and add an end card that says "Get your free audit". Export each in 9:16 and 4:5, named "A - [short ad name]". Then write a B version of each with a different hook from the same video, named "B - [short ad name]". List every file you made.

Step 4: Read the results

  • Give it a week before judging. Then look at these, in this order:
NumberWhat it tells youIf it's bad
Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions)Is the first line stopping the scroll?New hook, same video
Click-through rateDoes the ad make them want the audit?New copy or a stronger problem
Landing page conversionDoes the audit page close the deal?Fix the page, not the ad
Cost per leadWhat an audit request costs youJudge it against what a client is worth
Cost per booked callThe number that matters mostFix the follow-up (Session 4)
  • Turn off what doesn't work. Put the budget on what does. Make a new B version of every winner
  • Your ad performance belongs next to the ads themselves, so you can see which creative to make more of
Claude prompt
Here's my Facebook ads data for the last 7 days: [export or paste]. For each ad, show me hook rate, click-through rate, cost per lead and leads. Rank them by cost per lead. Tell me which to turn off, which to put more budget behind, and what new B version to make for each winner. Keep it to a short table and 3 recommendations.
