SEO Tools for Agencies: The 12 That Run Client Work (2026)

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Jan 13, 2022
Updated: Nov 14, 2026
Read: 11 min

Most agencies pay for more SEO tools than they use.

Three keyword tools. Two rank trackers. A content grader nobody opens.

Twelve tools cover the client work an SEO agency actually does. This post counts them down, from #12 to the one that matters most, with the real price and a screenshot of each.

What are the best SEO tools for agencies? The core stack is Google Search Console, Screaming Frog, Ahrefs and GA4. Add Claude for writing and analysis, Local Falcon and BrightLocal for local clients, Semrush as a second data source, Data Studio for reporting, ClickUp for project management, Surfer for content and Pitchbox for link outreach. Two of the four most important tools are free.

Prices below were checked on each tool's own pricing page in November 2026. Examples come from WEBRIS, a law-firm-only agency.

Here's the short version:

  • Start with the free tools. Search Console and GA4 are Google's own data. Everything else is an estimate.
  • Buy one crawler and one research tool. Screaming Frog and Ahrefs (or Semrush) cover most of the work.
  • Add local tools only for local clients. Map pack grids and citations are their own job.
  • The #1 tool costs nothing. Most agencies still don't use it enough.

#12: Pitchbox for Link Outreach

Pitchbox finds link prospects, sends the outreach emails and tracks every reply. It's the tool for agencies that build links in-house.

It sits at #12 because most agencies don't need it. Pro starts at $210 a month billed annually ($300 month to month). The Advanced plan most agencies would use is $420.

That only makes sense if you're sending hundreds of pitches a month. If links come from digital PR, local sponsorships or a vendor, skip it. More on where links come from in link building strategy.

#11: Surfer for Content Optimization

Surfer scores a draft against the pages that already rank and tells the writer what's missing. It's a check on the brief, not a replacement for one.

Surfer has moved to surfer.ai and now sells itself on AI search visibility as much as Google rankings. Standard is $99 a month billed yearly. A smaller Discovery plan starts at $49.

It's at #11 because a good content brief does most of the same job. Writers who follow a clear brief don't need a score to tell them what to cover.

#10: ClickUp for Project Management

ClickUp holds the tasks, deadlines and owners for every client. Without a project tool, the SEO plan lives in someone's head.

Business is $17 per user a month billed yearly ($25 month to month). Core is $9. The free plan works for a solo agency.

The tool matters less than the setup: one list per client, one task per deliverable, and a due date on everything. How to set that up is in SEO project management.

#9: Data Studio for Client Reporting

Data Studio turns Search Console and GA4 data into a client dashboard. It's free, and it's what most agencies use before they buy a reporting tool.

If you knew it as Looker Studio, it's the same product. Google has renamed it back to Data Studio, the name it had before 2022.

It sits at #9 because a dashboard isn't a report. Clients still need someone to explain what changed and what's next. What to put in front of them is in what to include in a monthly SEO report, and the paid alternatives are in client reporting for agencies.

#8: BrightLocal for Citations and Reviews

BrightLocal audits a local client's citations, tracks local rankings and runs review requests. It's the back office for local SEO.

Pricing is per location. For one location, Track is $31 a month billed annually ($41 month to month), Manage is $40 and Grow is $49.

Use it to find inconsistent business listings and to keep a steady flow of new reviews, two of the factors in local SEO ranking factors. If you have no local clients, you don't need it.

#7: Semrush as a Second Data Source

Semrush does the same job as Ahrefs: keywords, competitors, backlinks and rank tracking. Most agencies only need one of the two.

Plans start at $139 a month. It earns a spot because a second data source is useful when the first one looks wrong.

And third-party data is often wrong. Semrush and Ahrefs estimate traffic and search volume from models, not from Google. Pick one as your main tool and check it against the other on the numbers that matter. The full comparison is in Ahrefs vs Semrush vs Moz.

#6: Local Falcon for Map Pack Rankings

Local Falcon checks a client's map pack ranking from dozens of points across a city. One rank number can't show that. A grid can.

A rank tracker checks from one location. A local business ranks differently on every block, because distance from the searcher is the #2 local ranking factor.

The Starter plan is $24.99 a month billed annually and includes 90,000 credits a year. WEBRIS runs a grid in every law firm proposal, because it shows a prospect exactly where they disappear.

#5: Claude for Writing and Analysis

Claude drafts content, writes briefs and analyzes exports from every other tool on this list. It's the tool that saves the most hours per dollar.

Pro is $17 a month with annual billing, or $20 month to month. It connects to apps like Google Drive, so it can work on real client files instead of pasted snippets.

Use it for first drafts, briefs, crawl and keyword analysis, and client emails. Keep a person on every final draft. How that fits into delivery is in the AI SEO process.

#4: GA4 for Traffic and Conversions

GA4 shows what happens after the click: sessions, engagement and conversions by channel. It's the only tool here that ties SEO to leads.

It's free. Look at the last row in the screenshot: GA4 now reports AI Assistant as its own channel, so you can see visits from ChatGPT and other assistants without building a custom filter.

Two settings to fix on day one for every client:

  • Data retention. GA4 keeps event-level data for 2 months by default. Set it to 14 months.
  • Key events. Mark calls, forms and bookings as key events, or there's nothing to report as a lead.

Tracking AI traffic in more depth is covered in AI visibility tracking.

#3: Ahrefs for Keyword and Link Research

Ahrefs is the research tool: keyword volumes, competitor rankings, backlinks and content gaps. If you only buy one paid SEO tool, buy this or Semrush.

Plans start at $129 a month. Use it to size the keyword opportunity in a proposal, find what competitors rank for, and see who links to them. The workflow is in how to do keyword research.

I'll be honest: its traffic estimates can be badly wrong. On one WEBRIS client, Ahrefs showed organic traffic more than doubling while GA4 showed it falling. That's why Ahrefs is #3, not #1. The details are in why Ahrefs and Semrush traffic estimates are wrong.

#2: Screaming Frog for Technical Audits

Screaming Frog crawls a site the way Google does and lists every broken page, redirect, canonical and noindex tag. Most technical SEO problems show up in one crawl.

It's free for up to 500 URLs. A licence is £199 a year per user and removes the limit.

Technical SEO mostly comes down to one question: can Google crawl, index and rank the pages? Screaming Frog answers it. It finds 404s, redirect chains, wrong canonicals and pages tagged noindex by mistake. It's the first step in a website quality audit.

#1: Google Search Console

Google Search Console is the most important SEO tool an agency has. It's Google's own data on what it indexed, what it shows and what people click. And it's free.

Every other tool on this list estimates. Search Console reports. It's the only place that tells you, from Google, which pages aren't indexed and why.

The Page indexing report above is where most ranking drops start: redirects, 404s, duplicate canonicals and noindex tags. The Performance report shows real clicks, impressions and positions for every query.

Open it before you open anything else. Most agencies don't, and it's the #1 tool on this list.

What an Agency SEO Tool Stack Costs

The core stack costs about $170 a month: Search Console, GA4, Screaming Frog, Ahrefs and Claude Pro. Everything else depends on the clients you serve.

Add Local Falcon and BrightLocal when you sign local clients. Add Pitchbox when you build links in-house at volume. Most solo agencies never need all twelve.

SEO Tools for Agencies FAQs

What is the best SEO tool for agencies?

Google Search Console. It's free and it's Google's own data on indexing, clicks and queries. For a paid tool, Ahrefs or Semrush covers keyword, competitor and backlink research.

Do agencies need both Ahrefs and Semrush?

No. Most agencies need one. A second is useful as a check, because both tools estimate traffic and search volume rather than measure it.

Are Ahrefs and Semrush traffic estimates accurate?

Not always. Estimates can rise while real GA4 traffic falls. Use Search Console and GA4 for a client's actual traffic, and the paid tools for competitor research.

What free SEO tools should an agency use?

Google Search Console, GA4 and Data Studio are free. Screaming Frog is free for up to 500 URLs, which covers most small business sites.

How much do SEO tools cost for an agency?

A core stack of Search Console, GA4, Screaming Frog, Ahrefs and Claude Pro costs about $170 a month. Local and link building tools add $25 to $420 a month each.
