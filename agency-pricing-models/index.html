SEO Pricing Models: 7 Ways to Price Your Agency Services

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Nov 6, 2026
Last updated: Nov 6, 2026
Read: 14 min

There are at least 19 ways to price agency work. Somebody is running an agency on every one of them today.

You don't need 19. You need to answer two questions:

  1. Does this price make me the profit I need?
  2. Can the prospect understand it without shutting down?

Here are the seven SEO pricing models that matter, who carries the risk in each, what we actually charge at WEBRIS, and how to set the number.

What's the best pricing model for an SEO agency? One flat monthly retainer for a defined scope, with deliverables as a floor, not a cap. Set the number from three inputs: your cost floor, the market band and the client's value ceiling.

Here's the short version:

  • Every pricing model is really a decision about who carries the risk.
  • For SEO, sell one flat monthly retainer. No tiers, no hourly, no cheap starter audits.
  • For paid search, charge a flat fee, then a percentage of spend past a threshold.
  • Set the number with three inputs: your floor, your market, and their value.
  • Never price on a number you can't audit yourself.

What Is an SEO Pricing Model?

An SEO pricing model is how an agency structures what it charges: by the hour, by the project, by the month, by results, or by a share of the client's business.

The model is how you sell the price. It isn't the price. Two agencies can both sell a monthly retainer, one at $1,500 and one at $8,000.

So this post covers both: which model to use, and then how to land on the number.

Every Pricing Model Is a Decision About Risk

The same price can be sold four ways. The difference is who carries the risk, and that's what you're paid for.

You sell Looks like Risk sits with The catch
Time Hourly, day rate, hours on retainer Them Getting faster earns you less
Scope Project fee, monthly retainer, tiers You, on delivery Scope creep, and they count deliverables
Performance Percent of spend, per lead, per client signed You, on the result You need to see their numbers to get paid
Ownership Revenue share, profit share, equity You, on the business Their pricing and close rate are now yours

Margin rises as you move down the table. So does how much of their business you need to see.

Read that again. Never price on a number you can't audit yourself.

The 7 SEO Pricing Models

Here are the seven, with our take on each.

1. Monthly Retainer

One flat fee a month for a defined scope of work. It's the standard in our industry, which is exactly why it works: clients expect it and almost never question it.

Our take: this is what we sell for SEO.

I've been asked once whether month three should cost less than month one. I said we could charge more up front if that made them more comfortable. They said no, that's fine.

A retainer is an average. It's the cost to retain you.

One rule: treat deliverables as a floor, not a cap. "At least 20,000 words of content a month," not "20,000 words."

2. Project and Sprint Pricing

A flat fee for a defined piece of work: an audit, a migration, a website. Clear scope, easy to start.

Our take: fine for one-off work, bad as a way into SEO.

We used to run a "wedge offer": a $500 website quality audit, then upsell the technical work. Most people walked away. You do the same work, then cut off the future work, because SEO can't show results inside a project window.

Sign fewer clients, on retainer. In aggregate, it's always more revenue.

Paid search is different. "First month free, then a retainer once the leads come in" can work, because ads produce leads fast.

3. Hourly and Day Rate

You bill for time. No scope risk for you.

Our take: don't. Show a client hours and they'll ask for your timesheet in month three. Now you're selling time for money, and getting faster earns you less.

Track time internally so you know your costs. Never sell it.

4. Productized Tiers

Good, better, best. Low, middle, high.

Our take: no tiers. The client picks the cheapest, then asks if it'll still work. You say yes.

You just talked yourself out of money, and maybe out of results, because less work means less performance.

You're not a software company. You audited their business and looked at the competition.

Recommend one plan. That's the job.

5. Performance-Based Pricing

Pay per lead, pay per signed client, or a base fee plus a bonus.

Our take: not for SEO. Too much depends on things you don't control, like their ability to close.

Pay per lead breaks the first month lead quality dips: "the leads were garbage, I'm not paying." It can work for e-commerce, where you influence the conversion, or for paid search, if you've seen their sales process and can attribute the revenue.

6. Percentage of Ad Spend

For paid search: a percentage of what the client spends on ads.

Our take: a percentage on its own underpays you on small budgets. We charge a flat retainer, then switch to a percentage once spend crosses a threshold.

One account we just closed spends $50,000 a month across platforms. Our fee is $5,000 a month. The full structure is in how to price PPC management.

7. Ownership: Revenue Share, Profit Share, Equity

A share of the client's revenue, profit or business.

Our take: rare. Equity is more interesting than profit share, because with profit share you're relying on them to manage their expenses.

I like getting paid up front.

What We Actually Charge, and Why

Here's how we price at WEBRIS today.

  • SEO: one flat monthly retainer. Usually month to month. When month one is heavy, like a new website build, we ask for six months.
  • Paid search: a flat fee, then a percentage of spend. No charge per campaign. We run as many as it takes.
  • Both, when they're qualified. SEO plus paid is our favorite deal to sell. Those clients last the longest, because when one channel dips, the other carries it.

I'll be honest: SEO plus paid is only about 10% of the deals we send, because the client has to be ready to spend.

How we present that price in a proposal is in how to write an SEO proposal.

How to Set Your Price: Floor, Band, Ceiling

The model is how you sell the price. The number comes from three places.

The Floor: Your Cost Plus Margin

What it costs you to deliver the work, mostly people, plus the margin the business needs to run.

It tells you the absolute minimum. Never quote below it. Never show it to a client. It has nothing to do with what the work is worth.

The Band: Market Rates

The going rate in your niche. Secret shop competitors, ask peers in the vertical, and watch what gets signed.

It tells you the range a prospect is comfortable with. But it should never dictate your price. Pricing to the market is a race to the bottom that destroys margins.

The Ceiling: Value-Based Pricing

What bringing them new business is worth, times the market share actually available to them.

A new personal injury client is worth at least $15,000 to a law firm. A pressure washing job is a few hundred dollars. Same SEO work, very different ceiling.

That's why your niche matters so much. Value-based pricing needs their numbers and the market data, like search volume, to back it up. More in 3 agency niches that generate millions.

Where Your Price Lives

Floor from your costs. Band from your niche. Ceiling from their economics. Your price lives in the gap between the band and the ceiling.

Agency Pricing Mistakes

  • ❌ Hourly billing for agency services.
  • ❌ Three tiers so the client can pick the cheapest.
  • ❌ Cheap starter audits as a way into SEO retainers.
  • ❌ Pricing to the market instead of the client's value.
  • ❌ Performance pricing on SEO when you can't see their sales process.
  • ❌ A percentage of spend with no floor on small accounts.
  • ❌ Charging per campaign for paid search.

SEO Pricing Model FAQs

What is the best pricing model for an SEO agency? A flat monthly retainer for a defined scope. It's standard, clients understand it, and it's almost never questioned. Treat the deliverables as a floor, not a cap.

Should agencies offer pricing tiers? We don't. Clients pick the cheapest tier and ask if it will still work. Audit the business and recommend one plan.

Do cheap audits help sell SEO retainers? In our experience, no. We sold $500 website quality audits and tried to upsell the work after. Most people walked away.

Should SEO be priced on performance? Rarely. Results depend on things you don't control, like the client's ability to close. Performance pricing fits paid search better, and only when you can see the sales process and attribute the revenue.

What about points-based pricing? Points-based pricing gives each deliverable a point value and lets the client spend a monthly point budget. It adds flexibility, but it turns every month into a negotiation over deliverables. We'd rather sell one retainer with deliverables as a floor.

How do you set a price for SEO services? Start from your floor (cost plus margin), check the band (what your niche pays), then price toward the ceiling (what one new customer is worth to them).
