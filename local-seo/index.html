Local SEO Ranking Factors: The 6 That Drive Map Pack Results

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Aug 26, 2024
Updated: Nov 12, 2026
Read: 11 min

Most lists of local SEO ranking factors are 50 items long.

Nobody can act on 50 things.

Google names three ranking factors for local results. Everything you can actually work on rolls up into six.

What are the local SEO ranking factors? Google says local results are ranked on relevance, distance and prominence. In practice, the factors you can influence are the Google Business Profile, reviews, localized website content, local backlinks, location pages, and consistent citations across other platforms. Distance to the searcher matters too, but you can't change it.

Here's what each factor is, how to work on it, and what's against the rules, with examples from law firm campaigns at WEBRIS.

Here's the short version:

  • Google Business Profile. The primary category is often the single biggest lever.
  • Reviews. Google says more reviews and positive ratings help. Ask everyone, pay no one.
  • Localized content. Pages that match the service and the place.
  • Local backlinks. Relevance to the area beats raw authority.
  • Location pages. Real pages for real service areas, not city-swapped templates.
  • Citations and platforms. One identical name, address and phone number everywhere.

What Are Local SEO Ranking Factors?

Local SEO ranking factors are the signals Google uses to decide which businesses show up in the map pack and local results for a search. The map pack is the box of three businesses with a map that shows up for searches like "car accident lawyer near me."

Google's own Business Profile help page names three:

  • Relevance: "how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for."
  • Distance: "how far each business is from the customer who's searching."
  • Prominence: "how well-known a business is."

Google also says "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking."

Each of the six factors below improves either relevance or prominence. That's the whole game.

Distance: The Factor You Can't Change

Distance is how close the business is to the person searching, and it's why a business can rank first next to its office and not show up three miles away. No amount of optimization moves the office.

That's also why checking rankings from the client's office lies to you. You see their best position, not their real one.

The six factors below are how a business shows up farther from its front door. When distance sets a hard ceiling, Local Service Ads and PPC fill the gap.

1. An Optimized Google Business Profile

The Google Business Profile is the listing the map pack is built from, and its primary category is often the single biggest ranking lever. It tells Google what the business is, which drives relevance for every search.

Look at the grid above. Almost every winning profile shared a primary category the prospect didn't have. No amount of content fixes that. The category does.

What to get right on the profile:

  • Primary category that matches the money keyword and what the top profiles use. Secondaries for other services.
  • Business name exactly as it appears in the real world.
  • Description up to 750 characters, localized, ending with the address and a call to action.
  • Services listed one by one, each with a description and a link to its page.
  • Photos of the team, the office inside and out, and each service.
  • Posts written for each office, not the same post copied to every listing.

On the name, don't copy competitors who stuff keywords into it. Google's guidelines say the name "should reflect your business's real-world name" and that adding unnecessary information "could result in the suspension" of the profile.

I know what you're thinking. "Best Car Accident Lawyer Miami" ranks. Until it gets suspended. Don't bet a client's biggest lead source on it.

One more rule. A business that serves customers at their location and doesn't take visitors should hide its address and list service areas instead, per the same guidelines.

2. Consistent and Positive Reviews

Reviews are a confirmed local ranking factor: Google says "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." They also decide which of the three businesses in the map pack gets the call.

What matters is a steady flow, not a one-time push. In the grid above, the leaders had hundreds to thousands of reviews. That's the benchmark the client is up against.

The system we run for clients:

  • Ask every customer when the job closes, by text, about 30 minutes after, with a direct link to that office's review page.
  • Remind once a day later, then stop.
  • Respond to every review within 48 hours, positive and negative, without sharing details of the customer's case.

Now the rules, because this is where agencies get clients in trouble.

Google's review policy says businesses can't "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." That rules out review gating, where only happy customers get the link. Google's incentive policy also bans reviews influenced by a payment, discount or any other benefit.

On August 14, 2024, the FTC finalized a rule banning fake reviews, including paying for reviews that say something positive (or negative) and suppressing bad ones. It lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators.

Read that again. Ask everyone. Pay no one. Filter nothing.

3. Localized Content That Matches Search Intent

The website has to back up the profile with content about the service and the place, because Google reads it to judge relevance. The profile links to these pages.

A personal injury firm in Miami needs pages about personal injury in Miami. Not generic personal injury information that could be from any city.

What that looks like:

  • One strong page per service, targeting "[service] + [city]."
  • The city and neighborhoods used naturally in headings and copy.
  • Results, reviews and examples from the local area.
  • Answers to the questions people in that market actually ask.
  • LocalBusiness schema with the address and service area.

Which pages exist, which rank and which are missing is what a website quality audit tells you. For finding the searches, see keyword research.

4. High-Quality Local Backlinks

Local backlinks build prominence, and links from the business's own area count for more than links from big sites with no local connection. A link from the city's chamber of commerce beats a national blog.

The sources that work:

  • Chambers of commerce and business associations.
  • Sponsorships of local teams, events and charities.
  • Local news coverage and community publications.
  • Industry associations and niche directories.
  • Partnerships with complementary local businesses.

Brand mentions count too. In an Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands (May 2025), branded web mentions correlated more strongly with showing up in Google's AI Overviews than backlink counts did.

The full framework is in our link building strategy.

5. Location Pages for Every Service Area

A business with several offices or a wide service area needs a dedicated page for each location it actually serves. It's how the site becomes relevant for searches in cities beyond the main office.

Each page needs the office's name, address and phone number, a map, and content that's actually about that city. Results, reviews, details a customer there would want.

The fastest way to get pages Google ignores is a template with only the city name swapped. Google's spam policies call pages built only to rank for similar searches doorway abuse.

For service-area businesses without an office in every city, use a hub and spoke setup. The main service page is the hub. Each city page links back to it.

6. Consistent Citations Across Local Platforms

Citations are listings of the business's name, address and phone number on other sites, and they need to be identical everywhere. Mismatches send Google mixed signals about who and where the business is.

Start with the platforms that rank on their own: Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, the Better Business Bureau and Foursquare. Then the niche ones. For law firms, Avvo, Justia and FindLaw. For home services, Angi, Houzz and Thumbtack.

Then audit every listing. Fix the wrong ones, remove duplicates, and only then build the missing ones.

Watch the phone number. On a lot of profiles we audit, the main number on Google is a call tracking line. The real local line is the one that matches every directory, and it's the one that belongs in citations.

Submitting to the data aggregators, like Data Axle and Foursquare, pushes the business data out to hundreds of smaller sites, so you don't submit each one by hand.

What Isn't a Ranking Factor

You can't pay Google for a better local ranking, and the shortcuts people sell carry real risk.

  • ❌ Paying for map pack placement. Google says there's no way to pay for a better local ranking. Local Service Ads and Google Ads are ads, labeled as sponsored.
  • ❌ Keywords in the business name. Against Google's guidelines, and it can get the profile suspended.
  • ❌ Bought or gated reviews. Banned by Google, and the FTC can fine knowing violators.
  • ❌ City-swapped location pages. Doorway abuse under Google's spam policies.
  • ❌ Rankings checked from the office. Not a factor, just a misleading number.

How to Measure Local Ranking Factors

Measure the map pack on a grid of points across the service area, for each money keyword and each office. That's the only way to see distance at work and whether the other factors are pushing the business farther out.

We use Local Falcon for the grid scans. The numbers to watch:

  • Average rank position (ARP) across the grid.
  • Share of local voice (SoLV): the share of grid points where the business is in the top 3.
  • Review count and rating per office, next to the competitors.
  • Calls and direction requests from the profile.

Then tie it to calls, leads and customers. More in what to include in a monthly SEO report.

Local SEO Ranking Factors FAQs

What is the most important local SEO ranking factor?

Distance to the searcher has a big effect, but it can't be changed. Of the factors you can change, the Google Business Profile's primary category is often the biggest single lever, followed by reviews.

What are Google's three local ranking factors?

Relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches the search, distance is how far the business is from the searcher, and prominence is how well-known the business is.

Do reviews affect local rankings?

Yes. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking. Reviews can't be paid for or selectively solicited from happy customers.

Do backlinks matter for local SEO?

Yes, as part of prominence. Links from local, relevant sites like chambers of commerce, sponsorships and local news count for more than links from unrelated national sites.

Can you pay for a better ranking in Google Maps?

No. Google says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Paid placements like Local Service Ads are ads, shown and labeled separately.
