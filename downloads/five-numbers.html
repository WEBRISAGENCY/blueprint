The 5 numbers to get before you propose an ad budget

Ask these on the discovery call. They tell you what the prospect can pay per customer, and whether a paid search test is worth running at all.

Ask on the discovery call
1
What does an average new customer contribute?

After the direct costs of serving them. This sets the ceiling on what they can pay for a customer.

$
2
What percentage of inquiries become customers?

Their close rate. Pressure-test it. Most businesses think they close more than they do.

%
3
How many more customers can you handle?

Per month. If the answer is "unlimited," ask for a real number. Unlimited customers means an unlimited budget.

per month
4
Who answers the calls and follows up?

Including missed calls and weekends. If the owner takes every call himself, fix intake before you launch.

5
How much can you afford to risk on a test?

The budget they can lose without it hurting the business.

$
Then build the equation
Ad budget÷Cost per click→Clicks × landing page rate→Leads × close rate→Cost per customer

Compare the result to what a new customer is worth to them. Never send a proposal above their target cost per customer.

Can the budget teach you anything?
Enough to learn from

15 leads × $50 each ≈ $750 in media. 15 leads × $150 each ≈ $2,250. Fifteen is a planning example, not a magic number.

Too small to learn from

$500 budget − $300 fee = $200 for ads. At $100 a lead, that's about 2 leads. Don't split it across two channels.
