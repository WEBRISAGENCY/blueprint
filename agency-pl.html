Before We Dive In

HOW TO CLASSIFY
YOUR COSTS CORRECTLY

COST OF GOODS SOLD
If you can point to a specific client account and say "this cost exists because of that client" — it belongs in COGS.
Contractors doing client work — writer for Client A, link builder for Client B
Software tied to a client account — rank trackers, local SEO platforms, PPC tools billed per account
Link purchases — bought specifically for a client campaign
Call answering services — running on behalf of a specific client
OPERATING EXPENSES
If a person or tool serves the business as a whole — spread across accounts or working internally — it is an operating expense.
Executives and directors — an SEO director overseeing all accounts is not tied to one client
Project managers and account managers — managing across multiple clients, not doing deliverable work
Internal marketing and sales — generating new business, not serving existing clients
Shared software and infrastructure — CRMs, project management tools, reporting dashboards
THE TEST
Ask yourself: "If I lost this client tomorrow, would this cost go away?" If yes — COGS. If the cost stays regardless of any one client — operating expense.

The First Check

GROSS
PROFIT

70.77%

Healthy Range: 60% to 75%
What This Number Means
For every $100 in revenue, $70.77 remains after direct delivery costs. This is the pool that funds marketing, staff, software, and owner profit.
Why This Beats Net Income as a Signal
Gross margin reflects your business model. If it is low, no amount of expense cutting will save you. If it is healthy, you have real room to invest, grow, and pay yourself.
Warning Zone
Below 55% gross margin? Stop adding clients and fix your delivery model first. You are not scaling a business yet, you are scaling a loss.

WHAT FOUNDERS

The Mistake Everyone Makes
Most agency owners confuse net income with what they actually earn. In a founder-led agency, owner distributions come out before net income is calculated. Net income is not your income. It is what is left after you are already paid.

Profitability Killers

WHERE AGENCIES
BLEED MONEY

01
Over-Spending on Ads Before Revenue Supports It Marketing at 9.4% of revenue here. Healthy target is 3% to 5%. Every point above that is profit walking out the back door.
02
Hiring Too Fast, Pricing Too Low Agencies hire to support revenue that does not exist yet, or raise headcount without raising prices. COGS creeps above 40% before anyone notices.
03
Software Sprawl At 3.75% of revenue, a $1M agency is spending $37,500 a year on tools. Audit your software stack quarterly. Eliminate anything without a direct revenue tie.
04
Not Tracking COGS Per Client Blended COGS looks fine until you realize two clients cost 50% to serve and three cost 15%. You cannot fix what you cannot see at the client level.
05
Discounting to Close Every discount you give is a direct hit to gross margin. It is not an abstract negotiating tactic. It comes directly out of what you take home.
06
Confusing Revenue Growth With Profit Growth Adding $200K in revenue at the wrong margin can make you less profitable. Growth only works if the margin holds when you scale it.

Key Takeaways

3 THINGS TO DO
AFTER THIS

01
Know Your Gross Margin
Pull your own P&L. Calculate gross margin first. If it is below 60%, that is job one. Nothing else matters until you fix how you deliver relative to what you charge.
02
Set a Real Owner Comp Target
Decide what percentage of revenue you want to take home. Build your expense model backward from that number. Every other line item competes for what is left after COGS and your comp.
03
Audit Your Top 3 Expenses
Find your three biggest non-owner expense lines. For each one, ask: is this directly generating or protecting revenue? If the answer is neither, it is a candidate for cuts.
