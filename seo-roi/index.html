How to Calculate the ROI of an SEO Campaign

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Apr 2, 2019
Updated: Nov 7, 2026
Read: 13 min

"If I hire you, what do I get back?"

Every prospect asks it. Most agencies answer with "SEO is a long-term investment."

That answer loses deals. The prospect hears "I don't know."

What is SEO ROI? SEO ROI is the profit SEO produces compared with what it costs: (revenue from new organic customers minus the cost of SEO) divided by the cost of SEO. To forecast it before a client signs, project their organic traffic forward, convert that traffic into leads and customers at stated rates, put a dollar value on each customer, and compare it with your fee.

We do exactly that in every SEO proposal. This post walks through how the forecast is built, the data it uses, and how to turn it into an ROI number you can defend on a sales call.

Here's the short version:

  • Start from the prospect's real traffic, pulled from Ahrefs or Semrush. Never a made-up baseline.
  • Size the target from their competitors. What rivals already get is proof the traffic exists.
  • Back-load the curve. SEO is slow for months, then compounds.
  • State the conversion rates on the slide. 2% of visits become leads and 25% of leads become customers, unless the client gives you better numbers.
  • Run the downside case. If it still pays back at half the growth, you have a real offer.

What Is SEO ROI?

SEO ROI is the return a business gets from organic search, measured against what it spends on SEO. The formula is the same as any investment.

Two rules make the number honest:

  • Count only new revenue. The client was already getting some organic leads before you. Don't claim them.
  • Use profit per customer, not revenue, when you can. For ecommerce that's gross margin. For a law firm or service business, it's the fee the client keeps from each new customer.

Here's what that looks like at a glance: a client pays you $4,000 a month and SEO brings in $10,000 a month of new profit. That's ($10,000 minus $4,000) divided by $4,000, or 150% ROI.

The hard part isn't the formula. It's forecasting the revenue before any of it exists.

Why Most SEO ROI Forecasts Fall Apart

Most forecasts die on the first question the prospect asks: "Where did that number come from?"

Three things kill them:

  • An invented growth rate. "We'll grow you 40%" with nothing behind it.
  • Hidden conversion rates. The prospect can't check them, so they don't trust them.
  • A straight line. Real SEO growth is slow for months, then speeds up. A straight line promises results in month two that you won't deliver.

A forecast the prospect can audit beats a bigger number they can't.

Google says it plainly in its guide on whether you need an SEO: no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. So don't promise one. Show the math and label it directional.

How We Build the SEO Forecast in Every Proposal

The forecast is the last slide before Next Steps in every SEO proposal we build in Agency OS. It's called "What this is worth if it works," and it's built in six steps.

Every number on it comes from one of two places: the prospect's own data, or a rate printed on the slide. Nothing is hidden.

Step 1: Pull the Baseline Traffic

The baseline is the prospect's current monthly organic traffic. The proposal pulls it from Ahrefs or Semrush, along with two years of monthly traffic history.

Why a third-party tool and not Google Analytics? Because you don't have the prospect's analytics before they sign. Ahrefs and Semrush give you the same estimate for them and for their competitors, so the comparison is fair.

It's an estimate, and you should say so. What matters is that the prospect and their competitors are measured the same way.

If the tool returns zero traffic, the slide doesn't build. A forecast can't start from nothing.

Step 2: Size the Market From Competitors

Next, pull the same organic traffic number for each competitor in the proposal. This is the market ceiling: proof that the traffic is out there, because somebody's already getting it.

A competitor the tool returned nothing for is left out, not counted as zero. A zero would make the market look smaller than it is.

Picking the right competitors is its own skill. Here's our SEO competitive analysis method.

The smallest competitor matters most. If the weakest firm in the market gets 5,000 visits a month, 3,250 isn't a stretch.

Step 3: Set the Month-24 Target

The month-24 target is the higher of two numbers: four times today's traffic, or 65% of the smallest competitor's traffic.

Each rule covers a different prospect:

  • A tiny site in a big market gets 65% of the smallest rival. Four times 35 visits is still nothing, and the market supports far more.
  • A decent site in a small market gets four times today. That keeps the target from being too timid.

In the example we'll follow, the prospect gets 800 visits a month and the smallest competitor gets 5,000. Four times 800 is 3,200. 65% of 5,000 is 3,250. The target is 3,250 visits a month at month 24.

That's still below every competitor. Read that again: the forecast says "you could reach a bit more than half of what the weakest firm in your market already gets." That's an easy number to defend.

Step 4: Draw the Growth Curve

The curve from today to month 24 is back-loaded: slow in the first year, faster in the second. That's how SEO actually behaves. Pages take months to index, rank and earn links.

The math: each month gets the baseline plus the gap to the target, multiplied by (month ÷ 24) raised to the power of 1.6. You don't need to remember the exponent. What it does is put only about a third of the growth in year one, which is the conservative call.

The slide plots it every three months, and calls out month 12 as the year-one milestone.

Step 5: Draw the "If Nothing Changes" Line

The second line on the chart is what happens if the prospect does nothing. It drifts down 30% over 24 months.

That's not a scare tactic. Competitors keep publishing and earning links. A site that stands still loses positions to them.

The gap between the two lines is the whole argument. The cost of saying no isn't zero. It's the lower line.

Step 6: Turn Traffic Into Leads and Customers

Traffic becomes customers through two rates: visit-to-lead and lead-to-customer. The defaults are 2% of visits becoming leads and 25% of leads signing.

Both rates are printed on the slide, not buried in a spreadsheet. The rate is the part a prospect argues with, so we put it where they can see it.

If the client knows their own numbers, use them. You can change both rates in the proposal, and every figure on the slide moves with them.

In the example: 3,250 visits × 2% = 65 leads a month. 65 leads × 25% = 16 new customers a month by month 24, about 195 a year.

What the Forecast Slide Looks Like

Here's the slide the proposal builds from those six steps.

Four numbers across the top: traffic at month 24, the month-12 milestone, leads a month and customers a month. The chart shows both lines. The three notes underneath answer the three objections before the prospect raises them.

From Forecast to ROI: The Worked Example

The slide stops at customers a month. To get to ROI, you need two more numbers from the client: what a customer is worth, and what you charge.

For this example, assume each new customer is worth $5,000 to the client and the retainer is $4,000 a month. Use the client's real customer value. Ask for it on the discovery call.

We also only count customers above today's baseline, not above the "do nothing" line. That's the conservative version. It ignores the traffic you'd save from the decline.

When Does SEO Pay Back?

In this example, SEO covers its own monthly cost by month 5 and pays back everything spent by month 8.

By month 24, the client has spent $96,000 and earned about $596,000 in new customer value. That's an ROI of roughly 520% over two years.

That's why the forecast runs 24 months. Cut it at month 6 and SEO looks like a loss. Show the prospect when the lines cross.

Run the Downside Case

A forecast is only believable if it survives being wrong. So run it again with worse numbers.

Halve the growth, or halve the conversion rate, and it still pays back in month 12. Halve both and it pays back in month 19, with a 55% return over two years.

That's the same idea as our PPC ROI calculator: sell the downside case, not the best case. If the deal only works when everything goes right, it's not a good deal.

SEO ROI for Ecommerce Sites

Ecommerce swaps the lead chain for orders. Traffic × conversion rate × average order value × gross margin gives you new profit a month.

Use margin, not revenue. A $120 order at a 40% margin is $48 of profit, and that's what pays your retainer.

The traffic side doesn't change. Same baseline, same competitor ceiling, same back-loaded curve.

Where Each Input Comes From

Every number in the forecast has a source. Here's the full list.

Two of them come from the client, not a tool: what a customer is worth, and their real close rate. Get both on the discovery call. If they won't share them, use the defaults and say so on the slide.

How to Present the Forecast on a Sales Call

Don't lead with the big number. Walk them through how you got it.

The order that works:

  1. Their traffic today, and the two-year trend behind it.
  2. What their competitors get. This is usually the moment they lean in.
  3. The target, and why it's below every competitor.
  4. The rates, and an invitation to correct them. "Is 25% of leads signing about right for you?"
  5. The payback month, and the downside case.

When they correct a rate, change it and re-run it. A prospect who edits your forecast has bought into it.

More on running the call in how to sell SEO.

How to Measure Actual SEO ROI After They Sign

The forecast gets the deal. Measuring the real number keeps the client.

Once you have access, swap every estimate for a measured number:

  • Traffic: Google Analytics organic sessions instead of the Ahrefs estimate.
  • Leads: form fills and calls from organic, tracked as key events and in call tracking.
  • Customers: the CRM's lead source, so you count signed customers, not just leads.
  • AI search: AI referral traffic and leads who say an AI assistant sent them. Here's how to track AI visibility.

Report it monthly against the baseline from the proposal. Our SEO report template puts conversions and calls first for exactly this reason.

Compare the real line to the forecast line every quarter. Ahead of it is a renewal conversation. Behind it is a strategy conversation. Both beat silence.

SEO ROI Mistakes

  • ❌ Counting revenue the client already had. Only new customers count.
  • ❌ Inventing a growth rate. Tie the target to real competitors.
  • ❌ A straight-line forecast. SEO is back-loaded. Promise month 2 and you'll lose them in month 3.
  • ❌ Hiding the conversion rates. Put them on the slide.
  • ❌ Cutting the forecast at 6 or 12 months. Most of the payoff comes in year two.
  • ❌ Using revenue instead of profit for ecommerce.
  • ❌ Only showing the best case. Run the downside.
  • ❌ Never checking the forecast against reality after they sign.

SEO ROI FAQs

What is a good ROI for SEO? It depends on what a customer is worth to the client and what you charge. As a test, the forecast should pay back within 12 months in the downside case. If it only works when everything goes right, rethink the price or the scope.

How long does SEO take to show ROI? Usually longer than paid search. In our worked example, SEO covers its monthly cost by month 5 and pays back everything spent by month 8, but most of the return comes in year two. That's why we forecast 24 months.

How do you forecast SEO traffic? Start from the prospect's current organic traffic in Ahrefs or Semrush, set a month-24 target from what their competitors already get, and draw a back-loaded curve between the two. We use the higher of four times today's traffic or 65% of the smallest competitor's traffic.

What conversion rates should an SEO forecast use? Our defaults are 2% of visits becoming leads and 25% of leads becoming customers. Use the client's real rates when they share them, and print whichever rates you use on the slide.

Is there an SEO ROI calculator? The forecast in every Agency OS SEO proposal works as one: it pulls the prospect's traffic and competitors, sets the target, and converts traffic to customers. You can also run the math by hand with the formula in this post.

Can you guarantee SEO ROI? No. Google says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. Label the forecast directional, show where every number came from, and promise the work and the timeline instead.
