How To Run An SEO Competitive Analysis

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Jan 6, 2020
Updated: Nov 8, 2026
Read: 12 min

Your prospect doesn't care about their Domain Rating.

They care that the firm down the street gets the calls.

A competitive analysis shows them exactly who that firm is, and why it's winning. Done right, it's the slide that sells the whole campaign.

What is an SEO competitive analysis? An SEO competitive analysis compares a website with the competitors that win the searches it wants: their traffic, rankings, content, backlinks, map pack positions and AI citations. The goal is to find the specific gaps that explain why they win, and turn each gap into a task.

Here's the 8-step method, the same one behind the competitive slides in every SEO proposal we build.

Here's the short version:

  • Pick competitors by search results, not by who the client names. There are three sets: organic, map pack and AI answers.
  • Don't trust the tool's competitor list blindly. It mixes in directories, media sites and sites that only share a few keywords.
  • Lead with one rival of the same authority. If they get eight times the traffic, authority isn't the problem.
  • The content gap is usually the plan. Every service and city page that doesn't exist is a search someone else answers.
  • End with tasks, not a report. Every finding needs an action.

What Is an SEO Competitive Analysis?

An SEO competitive analysis measures a site against the competitors that rank for the searches it wants, to find out why they win. It's not a list of everything a competitor does. It's the shortlist of differences that cost the client customers.

Two jobs, one analysis:

  • For a prospect, it's the argument. It shows what they're losing and to whom.
  • For a client, it's the plan. Each gap becomes a page to build, a link to earn or a listing to fix.

Step 1: Pick the Right Competitors

Your SEO competitors are the sites that rank for your money keywords, not necessarily the businesses you compete with for deals. And there are three different sets of them.

  • Organic competitors: the sites in the regular results for your money keywords.
  • Map pack competitors: the businesses in the local 3-pack. For a local business, often a different list.
  • AI answer competitors: the brands ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI features name when someone asks for a recommendation.

Ask the client who they think they compete with. Then check. The firm they worry about is often not the one taking their searches.

Why the Tool's Competitor List Is Usually Wrong

SEO tools build competitor lists from shared keywords, so they mix in directories, media sites and sites that only overlap by accident.

Here's ours. Ahrefs lists the top "organic competitors" for theblueprint.training as a pricing-table plugin, a WordPress admin-bar plugin and a reporting tool. None of them sells agency training. They share between 6 and 10 keywords with us.

That's why the proposal builder in Agency OS follows three rules when it picks rivals:

  1. A list the strategist names always wins.
  2. Otherwise, it ranks Ahrefs' organic competitors by keywords in common.
  3. It drops directories, media sites and government or education domains before it keeps any.

It keeps three rivals by default, five at most. More than that and the slide stops being readable.

Pick One Rival at Your Level and Two Leaders

The most useful competitive set is one rival with about the same authority, plus two market leaders.

The same-authority rival kills the "we just need more links" excuse. If they have the same Domain Rating and eight times the traffic, the gap is content and targeting, not authority.

The leaders show the ceiling: how much traffic the market actually pays out.

Step 2: Compare the Topline Numbers

Put the client and each rival in one table: authority, ranking keywords, organic traffic and traffic value.

This is the competitive landscape slide from an Agency OS proposal, with sample data. The client row is highlighted, the best number in each column goes green and the worst goes red.

What each column tells you:

Traffic value is the one prospects remember. "Your rival's organic traffic would cost $41,000 a month in ads. Yours would cost $3,100" lands harder than any ranking.

Every number here is a third-party estimate. Use it to compare sites with each other, not as the client's real traffic. Here's how Ahrefs, Semrush and Moz compare as sources.

Step 3: Find the Content Gap

The content gap is the set of pages your competitors have and you don't. For a local business, it's usually service pages in each city they serve.

The proposal builds it as a grid: services down the side, markets across the top. A tick is a page that exists and ranks. A circle exists but doesn't rank. A dash was never written.

In this example the firm sells 5 services in 5 markets and has built 8 of the 25 pages. The 17 dashes are the content plan. It's finite, it's countable, and the client can watch it fill in.

Run a Keyword Gap Too

A keyword gap lists the keywords your competitors rank for and you don't. Ahrefs calls the tool Content Gap. Semrush calls it Keyword Gap.

Filter it before you read it:

  • Keep keywords where two or more rivals rank. One rival ranking can be a fluke.
  • Drop branded keywords for the competitors' names.
  • Group what's left by page. Twenty variations of one topic is one page, not twenty.

Then check search volume before you commit to anything. Our keyword research process covers the rest.

Step 4: Compare Authority

Compare referring domains, total backlinks and Domain Rating against the rivals, then work out what each link is returning.

The fourth number is the one most analyses skip: traffic per referring domain. Divide organic traffic by referring domains.

A low number means the links aren't the bottleneck. The site has authority it isn't using, usually because the pages to rank don't exist yet. A high number means content is working and links will multiply it.

Then look at where the rivals' best links come from. Link Intersect in Ahrefs shows sites that link to two or more competitors but not to you. That's your outreach list. More in our link building strategy.

Step 5: Check the Map Pack

For a local business, the map pack competitors matter more than the organic ones. That's where the calls come from.

Don't check one ranking from your office. Run a grid scan: the business's map pack position at dozens of points across the city. It shows who beats you in each neighborhood.

Then compare Google Business Profiles side by side:

  • Primary category. One of the biggest local ranking factors you control.
  • Reviews: count, average and how recent.
  • Photos, services and posts.

The proposal pulls grid scans from Local Falcon, and can import a Business Profile competitor export. Here's what else drives the map pack in our guide to local SEO ranking factors.

Step 6: Check AI Visibility Against Competitors

Check how often AI answer engines cite the client compared with each rival. A count on its own means nothing. Beside three competitors, it's a gap.

The proposal pulls AI citation counts from Ahrefs for the client and the same rivals as the competitor table, platform by platform.

If a rival gets cited in Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews and the client doesn't, look at the rival's cited pages. They're usually the pages that answer the question in the first paragraph.

AI answers vary from run to run, so treat this as a direction, not a score. Here's how much to trust AI visibility data.

Step 7: Read Their Top Pages

Open the five pages that bring each rival the most traffic and read them like a customer would. The tools tell you which pages win. Only reading tells you why.

For each page, note:

  • Does it answer the search in the first screen?
  • How deep is it? Sections, FAQs, pricing, process, local detail.
  • What proof is on it? Reviews, results, case studies, credentials.
  • How does it ask for the call?

You're not copying the page. You're finding the bar you have to clear.

Step 8: Turn the Analysis Into a Plan

Every finding needs an action, an owner and an order. An analysis that ends in a PDF changes nothing.

Order the work by how fast it produces leads:

  1. Map pack and Business Profile fixes. Fastest to move.
  2. Pages in striking distance. Keywords ranking 4 to 20 on pages that already exist.
  3. The content gap. New service and city pages, worst market first.
  4. Authority. Links to the pages that need them.

Need a starting point for the client's own site first? Run a website quality audit alongside the competitive analysis.

In a proposal, the competitive analysis is the argument. Three slides carry it.

The order matters. Who you lose to, what they have that you don't, and whether authority is the excuse. By the end, the scope of work reads like the obvious answer.

SEO Competitive Analysis Tools

You need one research tool, a grid tracker for local clients and a crawler. Everything else is optional.

SEO Competitive Analysis Mistakes

  • ❌ Using the client's list of competitors without checking.
  • ❌ Trusting the tool's competitor list blindly. Directories and plugins aren't rivals.
  • ❌ Comparing against giants only. Include a rival at the client's level.
  • ❌ Skipping the map pack for a local business.
  • ❌ Treating tool traffic as real traffic.
  • ❌ Reporting a keyword gap with 3,000 rows. Group it into pages.
  • ❌ Ending with findings instead of tasks.

SEO Competitive Analysis FAQs

How do I find my SEO competitors? Search your money keywords and note who ranks in the regular results, the map pack and AI answers. Then check a tool's organic competitors report, and drop directories, media sites and sites that share only a handful of keywords.

How many competitors should I analyze? Three: one with about the same authority, and two market leaders. Up to five if the market is crowded. More than that and the comparison stops being readable.

What should an SEO competitive analysis include? Traffic, ranking keywords and traffic value; the content gap; backlinks and referring domains; map pack positions for local businesses; AI citations; and a manual read of the rivals' top pages. End with a prioritized task list.

What's the best tool for SEO competitive analysis? Ahrefs or Semrush for organic and backlink data, a grid tracker like Local Falcon for the map pack, and a crawler for site structure. See our comparison of Ahrefs, Semrush and Moz.

How often should I do a competitive analysis? Once before the campaign starts, then a lighter check every quarter. Competitors publish and earn links all year.
