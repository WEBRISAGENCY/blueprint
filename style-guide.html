Purpose of This Guide

This Style Guide ensures that every piece of Blueprint Training content — written, visual, digital, or spoken — carries a consistent identity that is compelling, credible, and unmistakably ours. It should be used by any internal or external resource creating branded messaging on behalf of Blueprint Training.

Brand Image

Blueprint Training's brand does not exist in a vacuum. It is dynamic, and is built every time we publish a video, send an email, post on LinkedIn, or onboard a new member. In the nine years Blueprint has operated, it has built a steady reputation for radical transparency, practitioner-grade content, and systems that actually work — all with the member's results first.

To ensure a unified brand voice, a common language must be adopted for all brand communications. This guide is that language.

Voice & Tone The Blueprint Training Style Guide  ·  06
Voice
vs. Tone

Think of voice as the constant and tone as the variable. Our voice is the identity of Blueprint Training — it doesn't change. It's built from nine years of practitioner credibility and a founder who still runs an active agency every day.

Tone, however, adapts to context. The tone in a program launch email is energetic and direct. The tone in a reply to a struggling member is patient and encouraging. Both are still unmistakably Blueprint — but they read differently in the moment.

Always ask: Is this how a brilliant, no-BS practitioner would explain this to a peer? If yes, you're on brand.

Voice & Tone The Blueprint Training Style Guide  ·  09
Tone
Expressing the Voice

Blueprint speaks to agency owners who are smart, busy, and skeptical of fluff. Every message should respect their intelligence, get to the point fast, and leave them with something immediately actionable. Appeal to both logic and proof.

✓ OK
  • Specific, actionable language — name the exact tactic
  • First-person perspective from real agency experience
  • Lead with the outcome the member cares about
  • Everyday, conversational phrases
  • Emphasis on expertise, track record, and results
  • Short sentences. Plain words. No jargon padding.
"Here's exactly how we're using Claude to automate prospecting inside Webris — step by step, including the prompts."
"This took us 3 hours to build. Now it runs itself. Here's the full breakdown."
✗ NOT OK
  • Hyperbole or boastful phrasing without proof
  • Sales-y, cheesy language
  • Phrasing that puts brand needs at the forefront
  • Transactional or manipulative language
  • Vague aspiration without specifics
  • Content that could have been written by anyone
"Are you leaving money on the table? Join our REVOLUTIONARY AI-powered methodology and TRANSFORM your agency TODAY!"
"In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, agencies must leverage best-in-class solutions to remain competitive."

Stylistic
Rules
01
Be Concise
Online visitors scan, they don't read. Use short sentences that stick to one idea. Leverage bullets, subheadings, and whitespace to make information quickly digestible. Less is more.
02
Lead With a Benefit
Use concepts that impact the reader emotionally or intellectually. Emphasize how Blueprint content will improve their agency. "Build a seven-figure solo agency" before "Here's our program."
03
Write in 2nd Person
Avoid "the member" or "students." Simply write "you" and "your." It's more direct, more human, and more respectful of the reader's intelligence. Blueprint speaks to one person at a time.
04
Brand Name Usage
"The Blueprint Training" on first reference, then "Blueprint." "Agency Build" for the membership product. "The One-Person Agency" for the flagship program. Never abbreviate to "BPT" in external content.
05
Print Rules
Out-of-home and print collateral compete for attention in the physical world. One simple headline and subhead is all the copy you can afford. Exception: close-inspection formats like brochures allow more detail.
06
Punctuation & Style
Use the serial comma. Em-dashes without spaces. Headlines in Title Case. Use "&" in headlines instead of "and." Periods on sentences in bullet lists. AP Style as the default fallback guide.

Brand Logo The Blueprint Training Style Guide  ·  13
What Our Icon Stands For

The Blueprint Training mark is a yellow square carrying a circuit-board node pattern — the visual language of precision engineering and systematic thinking. The grid lines and dot nodes reference the actual notation of a technical blueprint document.

The nodes form a deliberate path — representing the progression a member takes through the Blueprint Training system, from raw operator to systematized agency owner. Every node is a milestone. The path is the program.

The Orbitron wordstack communicates technical authority and future-forward thinking. "Blueprint" in gold sits above the quieter "Training" — the brand name dominates, the category descriptor recedes.

Common
Errors

Note: This is not a comprehensive list of errors. These are the most common and egregious violations of logo integrity.

✗ Do Not Stretch
Blueprint
Do not stretch, squash, skew, or distort the logo or mark in any way.
✗ Off-Brand Color
Blueprint
Do not edit the logo color, use an off-brand color, or reduce the logo opacity.
✗ Drop Shadow
Blueprint
Do not add graphic effects including drop shadows, glows, or outlines to the wordmark.
✗ Busy Background
Blueprint
Do not place the logo on a high-contrast pattern or busy background.
✗ Change Layout
Blueprint
Do not rearrange the relationship between the mark and wordmark elements.
✗ Encroach Clear Space
→
Always maintain clear space equal to the width of the mark on all four sides of the logo.

Visual Style The Blueprint Training Style Guide  ·  43
The Grid
System

All Blueprint layouts are built on an explicit grid. The yellow micro-grid background is both structural and decorative — it directly references the blueprint document aesthetic that the brand takes its name from.

Digital: 12-column responsive, 24px gutters, 6% side margins.
Presentation: 12-column landscape grid, left-weighted content zone.
Print: 4-column portrait grid with generous margins.

Background Grid
Yellow grid at 4–8% opacity on dark backgrounds. 60px cell size. Applied to hero sections, chapter openers, and full-bleed surfaces.

Blueprint photography should feel like it was captured inside a working agency — not a stock photo shoot. Dark environments, screen glow, real dashboards, working setups. If it could appear on a generic SaaS landing page, it's too polished.
