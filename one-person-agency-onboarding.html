This is the twelve month delivery plan onboarding hands off to, and the proof of the claim on the slide before it. Six sprints, and look at how little of it is mint. The human work is the kickoff call, technical implementation, the review system, content editing, manual link builds and managing vendors. Everything white is audit, analysis, drafting and reporting, which is the bulk of the hours in a traditional agency and almost none of the hours here. That ratio is the whole one person agency argument.

The Lightning Bolts

Four Steps That
Press A Button

Create folder

Makes the client's folder in your Drive, named after them, with your subfolders and starter documents already inside.

Write announcement

Writes the post that tells your team who signed, what they bought and what happens next. Keep fees out of it.

Send email

Opens the onboarding email written from your template, addressed to the client, carrying their portal link.

Open accesses

Opens their access sheet, tracks how much they have granted, and ticks itself off when everything is in.

The bolt sits on the subtask in every view, the card included. Press it and the step is done. Nothing fires on its own, because sending a client an email on a timer is how you email the wrong client.

Client · Plan · Onboard Client

Be clear that these are assisted, not autonomous. A human presses every one. That is deliberate. The access checklist is the one worth lingering on: it is the only step that completes itself, because it watches what the client has actually granted rather than what you remember asking for.

Everything else about the client lives in one Overview table you can click and edit: status, start date, contacts, scope, Drive folder, and any field you add yourself. Fees stay in the pipeline, where delivery does not see them.

Clients · Client Info

Step one is the one that quietly matters. The service tags are not decoration, they filter the access list and the login list. Tag a client Meta Ads only and nobody wastes a week chasing Search Console access they were never going to use. On the last line: retainer and billing live on the pipeline record, not here, so a delivery person opening a client never sees what the client pays.

The Client Portal

Their Workspace

Their address

A link of their own, with an optional passcode. One off links for anyone you want to show a single thing to without handing over the keys.

What they can see

Eight sections, each one on or off per client: Plan, Performance, Content, Links, Calls, Schedule, Logins, Accesses.

Read only, always

They open tasks, subtasks, files and comments exactly as your team sees them. They change nothing. Logins is the one place they write, never read.

Why this is the retention feature

Month one is audits, access and setup. None of it looks like anything from the outside. The portal is where the client watches the work happen, comment by comment, so you get credit for it while you are doing it instead of defending it on a call in month three.

Clients · Portal · Settings

Spend time here. The plan and content tabs are not a summary, they are the same cards your team works, with the same subtasks and the same comment threads. That is the whole argument: you are already doing the work and already writing the updates, so stop rewriting them into a status email. Tell the room to go look at a real client portal after this.

What Good Looks Like

The First Seven Days

Day 1

Folder built, team told, invoice out, portal live, onboarding email sent with the link in it.

Days 2 to 4

Access requests land and get granted. The checklist ticks itself off as they come in. Kickoff call booked from their portal.

Day 5

Data connected. Analytics, Search Console, ads and rankings reporting into the client record.

Day 7

Onboarding card closes. The service plan goes on. The client has already watched a week of work.

The one that breaks it

Access. Everything downstream waits on it, so chase it on day two, not day six. It is the only step in onboarding you cannot do for the client.

The One Person Agency

This is the slide to hold people to. Seven days is not aspirational, it is what the plan is built for, and six of the seven steps are yours to control. Access is the exception, which is exactly why it gets chased early and why the checklist tracks it instead of someone remembering.
