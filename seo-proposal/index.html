SEO Proposal: How To Build One That Closes Deals in 2026

Founder, The Blueprint Training

An SEO proposal is where most agencies lose the deal, before the call even starts.

Three pricing tiers. A menu of services. Twelve pages of jargon the prospect doesn't understand.

They read it, say "let me think about it," and you never hear from them again.

I've been selling SEO for 15 years. I'm going to show you the exact proposal we sent a law firm last month, slide by slide, with the firm's name taken out.

$4,000 a month, month to month. Twenty slides. Built in Agency OS in a few clicks.

You'll get all of it: the data, the 16 sections, the real numbers, a slide-by-slide SEO proposal template you can copy, the pricing math, how to pitch it, and how we build it.

What should an SEO proposal include? A diagnosis of where the prospect stands across the map pack, the website results and AI answers, built from external data like a crawl, Ahrefs and Local Falcon. Then one plan at one monthly price, and a clear next step.

Here's the short version:

  • An SEO proposal is a diagnosis, not a brochure. It shows the prospect where they stand, who's beating them, and exactly what you'll fix.
  • Build it from external data: a crawl, Ahrefs or Semrush, Local Falcon and AI search checks. You don't need their Google Analytics.
  • Show them every place customers find them: the map pack, the website results and the AI answers.
  • Recommend one plan at one monthly price. No tiers. No hourly.
  • Build it in minutes, not days. Speed wins deals.

What Is an SEO Proposal?

An SEO proposal is the document an agency sends a prospect to show where their search visibility stands today, what's holding it back, what the agency will do about it, and what it costs.

That's the textbook answer.

Here's the real one: a good SEO proposal is a diagnosis. The prospect should finish it understanding their own market better than they did before they opened it.

A bad SEO proposal is a brochure. It lists your services, your awards and your process, and says nothing about their business.

Look at the title of our proposal. It doesn't say "SEO Proposal." It says "Search, Local & GEO Analysis." That's what the prospect is buying: an analysis of their market. It's the same thinking as an SEO competitive audit, pointed at a sale.

Why Most SEO Proposals Lose the Deal

Most proposals break in one of three ways.

1. They're too slow. Every day between the discovery call and the proposal gives a competitor time to follow up first. Building a deep proposal by hand takes hours, so most agencies send it days later. (We fixed this with the Agency OS Proposal Builder.)

2. They have no depth. A prospect paying $4,000 or $8,000 a month wants proof you understand their market, their competitors and their rankings. A list of services doesn't prove that. If you want to land bigger agency clients, this is the bar.

3. They can't show what success looks like. Serious buyers want to see the gap and what closing it is worth. If you can't connect rankings to revenue, you're asking them to take it on faith.

Then there are the self-inflicted wounds:

  • ❌ Three pricing tiers, so they pick the cheapest one
  • ❌ Hourly line items, so they ask for timesheets
  • ❌ Auditing channels you don't even sell
  • ❌ Asking for Google Analytics access before they've said yes

Every one of those adds friction to a deal that should feel inevitable.

Search Changed. Your Proposal Has to Change With It.

You can't sell "SEO" the way you sold it five years ago. The clicks moved.

According to the Pew Research Center, Google users clicked a traditional search result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% when one didn't. That's from browsing data on 900 U.S. adults in March 2025.

Read that again. When the AI summary shows up, clicks to websites get cut nearly in half.

And only 1% of those visits clicked a link inside the AI summary itself.

The searching didn't stop. It spread out:

  • Google's AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly active users, per Alphabet's Q2 2026 earnings remarks (July 2026).
  • ChatGPT has more than 900 million weekly active users, per OpenAI (February 2026).

So if your proposal only shows organic rankings, you're showing the prospect one slice of a much bigger page.

The Six Places Your Prospect's Customers Find Them

We call it blended search, because a single search now returns six different things:

  1. Local Service Ads
  2. Traditional PPC ads
  3. AI Overviews
  4. The Google Maps pack
  5. Traditional website results
  6. ChatGPT, Claude and other AI platforms

Your SEO proposal covers the organic side: the map pack, the website results and the AI answers. It should show where the prospect appears in each one, and where their competitors appear instead.

That's what makes a proposal feel like a diagnosis. You're not selling them a channel. You're showing them the whole page they're losing on.

The 3 Rules of an SEO Proposal That Closes

Every slide in our proposal follows three rules. Break them and the structure doesn't matter.

Rule 1: It's a Consultation, Not a Brochure

Your proposal should help the prospect understand where they stand and what's happening around them.

That understanding is what earns the deal. Not your logo, not your team page, not your awards.

If a slide doesn't teach them something about their own business, cut it.

Rule 2: Only Audit What You'll Fix

Every issue you show should be one they'll pay you to solve.

Not selling Facebook ads? Don't audit their Facebook.

Not doing a site rebuild? Don't spend three pages on their design.

Every problem you raise without a fix is a loose end the prospect will pull on. It also makes your plan look incomplete.

Rule 3: The Pitch Matters More Than the Document

I'll be honest: nobody reads a 20-slide proposal cover to cover.

Get the document about 80% right. Then put your effort into the walkthrough. Present it live, on a call, with their search results open in another tab.

The document backs up what you said. The pitch is what closes.

What You Need From the Discovery Call First

A proposal is only as good as the call before it.

Before you build anything, you need five answers. These come straight from how we scope every deal:

  1. What is one new customer worth to you after the direct costs of serving them?
  2. What percentage of your inquiries become customers?
  3. How many more customers can you handle right now?
  4. Who answers the calls and follows up on new leads?
  5. How much can you afford to risk on a test before it has to pay off?

Questions 1 and 2 set your price ceiling. Questions 3 and 4 tell you if more leads will even turn into more revenue.

Log the answers on the lead in the Agency OS CRM, so they flow straight into the proposal.

If you don't have these answers, you're guessing. Our client questionnaire template has the full list we use.

Why You Don't Need Google Analytics or Search Console

Asking a busy owner for Google Analytics and Search Console access before they've signed slows the deal to a crawl.

They have to find the login. Then figure out how to add a user. Then forget.

Meanwhile, three other agencies follow up.

Skip it. External data is enough to show them the problem.

Even Google's own guidance frames an audit as an estimate. Its guide to hiring an SEO (updated June 2026) says an audit "should be about giving you realistic estimates of improvement, and an estimate of the work involved."

You'll get full access during onboarding, after they've said yes.

A Real SEO Proposal Example, Slide by Slide

Here's the proposal we sent a personal injury and civil rights firm in Atlanta in October 2026. I've taken the firm's name out. Everything else is real.

The setup: a firm with 401 Google reviews at 4.9 stars and record verdicts, close to invisible in search. Traffic down 83% from its peak. Zero map pack top-3 spots. Zero citations in ChatGPT.

That's the story the deck tells. Sixteen sections, twenty slides, in this order.

Two things to notice.

The competitor and AI sections do the emotional work. Nobody likes seeing the firm down the street beat them. That's where the prospect leans in.

Every audit section sets up a piece of the plan. Nothing is in there for decoration.

Now section by section.

1. Executive Summary

Two columns: what is happening, and where the opportunity is. Six bullets each, every one with a number.

Then one number across the bottom that sums up the gap. Here, a competitor with the same authority earns $62,379 a month in traffic value. This firm earns $655.

Write this last. You can't pick the angle until you've seen the data.

2. Current Performance

Show 24 months of organic traffic, with Google's updates marked on the chart.

This one tells a clear story: traffic peaked at 1,465 visits a month, started falling, then dropped hardest during the August 2025 spam update. Down 83%, with no recovery since.

Up, flat or down, the trend is your first talking point. Down means urgency.

After they sign, the same chart leads your monthly SEO report.

3. Competitive Landscape

Put them side by side with three real competitors. Pick one rival with the same authority and two market leaders.

The headline writes itself: same Domain Rating as the rival, one twenty-fourth of the traffic.

That kills the "we just need more links" objection before they raise it. The gap is how the site is built.

We pull this straight from our SEO competitive analysis process.

4. AI Citations

Show how often each AI platform cites them, next to the same three competitors.

This firm had 17 citations, all from Google AI Overviews. Zero from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot or AI Mode.

This is the section most agencies don't have. It's often the one that wins the deal, because the prospect has never seen it before.

5. Live ChatGPT Check

Then make it real. Ask ChatGPT the questions their customers ask, and screenshot the answers.

"Who is the best personal injury lawyer in Atlanta?" ChatGPT named five firms. This firm wasn't one of them.

A table of citation counts is data. A screenshot of ChatGPT recommending their competitors is a feeling.

6. GEO Audit

GEO (generative engine optimization) is the work of getting a site found, read, trusted and cited by AI answers.

We score the site on 10 checks: crawlability, direct answers, question coverage, evidence, expertise, consistency and more. This firm failed six.

It answers the question they'll ask after section 5: "What are they doing that we're not?"

7. Local Performance: One Grid Per Keyword

Run a Local Falcon grid across the service area for each core keyword. We ran three.

Most firms think they rank locally because they rank from their own office. The grid shows what customers a few miles away actually see. This firm held 0 of 75 top-3 spots.

Then list the top five competitors in the grid, with their Google Business Profile categories. Here, every leader used Personal injury attorney as its main category. This firm used Employment attorney. One setting, costing them the map pack.

Our local SEO guide covers how to fix what this section uncovers, and here's how to sell local SEO as its own offer.

8. Content Inventory and Top Pages

Count every page, then show which ones earn traffic.

107 pages. 87 get no traffic. 76 don't rank for a single search. Two pages bring in most of the visits.

That sets up the content pitch. Here's how we create SEO content that ranks.

9. Traffic by Service or Practice Area

Group the pages by what the business sells. Then show which service lines are invisible.

Here, 51 of 60 practice area pages get no traffic. The 17 whistleblower pages bring in 9 visits a month, combined.

10. Keyword and Funnel Analysis

Split their traffic by funnel stage: awareness, consideration, decision and brand.

This firm's traffic was ready to hire. But 77 of its 143 decision-stage visits came from one keyword. One ranking drop and the leads are gone.

The fix starts with keyword research by funnel stage.

11. Backlinks vs. Competitors

Don't just count links. Count the good ones.

This firm had more linking sites than its same-authority rival, but only 28 strong ones. The market leader had 173. That's the authority gap, in one row.

Our link building strategy explains what goes into fixing it, and how to get high-authority links for clients.

12. Technical SEO Audit

Keep it to the issues that matter, with the fix next to each one.

The big one here: the whole site existed twice, on www and non-www, with half the internal links pointing at the wrong copy. The homepage H1 read "NEWS AND REPORTS."

Most fixes were configuration, not rewrites. Say that. It tells the prospect results can come fast.

13. Keyword Opportunity

Now show what they could win. Real keywords, real search volumes, and where they rank today.

24,670 Atlanta injury searches a month. This firm was on page one for two.

We also split out the national whistleblower searches: 16,700 a month, with the firm on page one for one term.

14. Performance Forecast

This is how you fix failure mode #3: show what success looks like.

We don't invent a growth rate. The month-24 target comes from the prospect's own traffic and their competitors': the higher of 4× today, or 65% of the smallest competitor's traffic. That keeps it below every rival in the market.

The curve is back-loaded, because SEO is slow in year one and compounds in year two. A second line shows what happens if they do nothing: traffic drifting down as competitors keep publishing.

Traffic becomes leads and cases at rates printed on the slide: 2% of visits enquire and 25% of those sign. Label it clearly: directional, not a guarantee.

Here's the full method, and how to turn it into a payback month, in how to calculate the ROI of an SEO campaign.

15. Plan of Attack

Order the work by how fast it produces leads.

  • Weeks 1 to 4: Google Business Profile fixes, the duplicate site, lead tracking.
  • Months 2 to 3: Rebuild six case-type pages on low-competition keywords.
  • Months 4 to 6: The national whistleblower hub, authority and AI visibility.

Every step opens with a number from the audit. That's what makes the scope feel inevitable instead of padded.

Once they sign, the plan becomes sprints and tasks in Agency OS project management.

16. Investment

One price. Six workstreams laid out month by month, so they see exactly what happens when.

$4,000 a month. Month to month. 30 days notice.

Link placements are optional, billed per placement only when one is approved and live. That keeps the retainer simple.

Local SEO Proposal Template

Selling to a local business? Same template, three changes.

  1. Run a map grid for every core keyword, not just one. We ran three for this firm, one slide each.
  2. List the top five competitors in each grid with their Google Business Profile categories, ratings and review counts. Category mismatches are the fastest win you'll find.
  3. Run a grid for every location. A multi-location business gets a grid per office.

The car accident grid below found 74 of 75 top-3 spots going to firms with Personal injury attorney as their main category. That one slide sold the local workstream.

"87 of 107 pages get no traffic" isn't a deliverable. It's a problem the prospect can't unsee. The content workstream is the obvious answer to it.

That's the whole trick. Measure something, show it, then show the work that fixes it.

How to Price an SEO Proposal

Pricing is two problems at once.

Internally: does this price make you the profit you need? Externally: can the prospect understand it without shutting down?

You solve both with three numbers.

The Floor: Your Cost Plus Margin

Your floor is what it costs you to deliver the work each month, mostly people, plus the margin the business needs to survive a bad quarter.

Never quote below it. Never show it to a client.

It tells you the minimum. It has nothing to do with what the work is worth.

The Band: What Your Niche Already Pays

Contractors, dentists and law firms pay very different numbers for the same SEO work.

Find your band by secret shopping competitors, asking peers in the vertical, and watching what gets signed.

But don't let the band set your price. Pricing to the market is a race to the bottom. If you're not niched yet, start with how to pick a niche for your agency.

The Ceiling: What One New Customer Is Worth

This is where the real money is.

Ask the prospect what one new customer is worth on the discovery call. Then check there's enough search demand to deliver more of them.

A personal injury client is worth $15,000 or more to a law firm. A pressure washing job might be worth a few hundred dollars.

Same SEO work. Very different ceiling.

That's why your niche matters so much. Your price is a fraction of the value you can deliver.

Who Carries the Risk

Every pricing model is really an answer to one question: who carries the risk?

Margin rises as you move down the table. So does how much of their business you need to see.

Never price on a number you can't audit yourself. For the full breakdown, read our guide to agency pricing models.

Why We Send One Price

Here's what we do, and why:

  • ❌ No hourly. They'll ask for timesheets, and you're back to selling time.
  • ❌ No tiers. The client picks the cheapest one, then asks if it'll still work. You audited the business. Recommend one plan.
  • ❌ No performance pricing for SEO. Too much depends on things you don't control, like their ability to close.
  • ❌ No cheap starter audits. We tried selling a low-cost audit and upselling after. Most people walked.
  • ✅ One flat monthly retainer. Simple, standard, and almost never questioned.

I know what you're thinking: "Tiers give them a choice. Won't one price scare them off?"

It's the opposite. Three options tell the prospect you don't know what they need. One recommendation tells them you do.

How to Present the SEO Proposal on the Call

Never email a proposal and wait. Present it live.

The proposal is only as good as the pipeline feeding it. Here's how to generate predictable leads for your agency.

Here's the order:

  1. Open with the search results, live. Pull up their main keyword and walk through everything above the organic listings.
  2. Show where they stand: performance, competitors, AI and the map pack.
  3. Let the competitor and ChatGPT slides land. Pause. Let them react.
  4. Walk the plan, tying each step back to a number they just saw.
  5. Give one price and stop talking.

Frame it around outcomes, not channels. This is the line we use:

"Our goal isn't to sell you more marketing channels. It's to put you in more of the places your best customers are making decisions."

How to Handle the Objections

You'll hear the same four objections. Have the answers ready.

That second one comes straight from Google. Its guide to hiring an SEO says plainly: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google."

Use it. It makes you the honest one in a field full of promises.

Should You Add PPC and Local Service Ads to an SEO Proposal?

Usually, yes. That's what "blended search" means in practice.

SEO takes months to compound. Paid search and Local Service Ads get the phone ringing while that work builds. Early results buy you time with the client.

The line we use: "SEO is building your organic presence. PPC gives us another way to compete for priority searches while that work develops."

Be straight with them on one point.

Google says its search results are independent of its advertising programs. Ads don't help organic rankings. They're a parallel channel, not a shortcut.

One more rule: don't split a tiny budget across two paid channels just to call it blended. Two leads a month teaches you nothing.

Mistakes That Kill SEO Proposals

  • ❌ Sending it days after the call. Send it while you're still top of mind.
  • ❌ Leading with your agency. Lead with their problem. (Strong agency positioning does the selling before the proposal.)
  • ❌ Showing problems you won't fix. Every issue needs a matching workstream.
  • ❌ Using vanity metrics. Tie rankings to customers and revenue.
  • ❌ Promising rankings. Google says no one can guarantee them, and the prospect knows it.
  • ❌ Emailing it instead of presenting it. The document doesn't close. You do.
  • ❌ Copy-pasting the executive summary. If it fits any prospect, it convinces none.

SEO Proposal FAQs

What should be included in an SEO proposal? An executive summary, current performance, a competitor comparison, AI citations and a GEO audit, map pack grids, a content and keyword analysis, backlinks, a technical audit, keyword opportunities, a forecast, a plan of attack, and one monthly price.

How long should an SEO proposal be? Ours runs 20 slides. Length matters less than relevance: every slide should show the prospect something about their own business.

How long does it take to build an SEO proposal? By hand, most of a day. In the Agency OS Proposal Builder, about five minutes once Ahrefs and Local Falcon are connected.

Do you need Google Analytics access to write an SEO proposal? No. A crawl, Ahrefs or Semrush, Local Falcon and AI search checks are enough. Asking for access before they sign slows the deal down.

Should an SEO proposal include pricing tiers? We don't use them. Tiers push prospects to the cheapest option. One recommendation at one price signals you know what they need.

Can you guarantee SEO results in a proposal? No. Google states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. Show a forecast built from real data, label it directional, and promise the work and the timeline instead.

Is there a free SEO proposal template? Yes. The slide-by-slide outline above is the template we use. Blueprint members get the editable deck, and Agency OS fills it in with the prospect's data for you.

Can I send an SEO proposal as a PDF? Yes. Agency OS exports every proposal as a PDF. But present it live first, then send the PDF as the follow-up.

What's the difference between an SEO audit and an SEO proposal? An audit finds the problems. A proposal sells the fix. Ours is both: thirteen audit sections, then the forecast, the plan and the price.
