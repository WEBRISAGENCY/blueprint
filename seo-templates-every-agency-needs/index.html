SEO Templates: The 10 Every Agency Needs (With Examples)

Elena Dimova
Head of Operations, The Blueprint
Published: Nov 10, 2021
Updated: Nov 12, 2026
Read: 10 min

Every SEO client needs the same ten documents.

Agencies that rebuild them from scratch for each client lose hours a week, and the work comes out different every time.

Here are the ten SEO templates that run an agency, counted down from #10 to the one that matters most, with a screenshot of each.

What is an SEO template? An SEO template is a reusable document, spreadsheet or deck for a task that repeats on every client: discovery calls, onboarding, audits, keyword research, content briefs, project plans, reports and proposals. The questions, steps and layout stay the same. Only the client's data changes.

Here's the short version:

  • Templates follow the client lifecycle: sell, onboard, audit, plan, produce, report.
  • A good template is a checklist plus instructions, so anyone on the team gets the same result.
  • Audits and plans matter more than reports. They decide what work gets done.
  • The #1 template is the one that wins the client. Without it, the other nine never get used.

#10: SEO Kickoff Meeting Template

The kickoff meeting template is the agenda for the first call after the contract is signed. It turns a friendly intro call into a working session.

It covers four things:

  • Who's who: name, title and email for everyone on both sides.
  • Follow-ups on the onboarding answers: internal resources, past campaigns, target audience and past agency work.
  • Access: CRM or sales data, so results can be tied to revenue later.
  • How work gets shared: a screen share of the project folders and where to leave comments.

One question in the template does more than the rest: "Is there anything specific you'd like us to focus on when building your project plan?" The answer goes straight into template #2.

#9: Client Onboarding Questionnaire

The onboarding questionnaire collects what the agency can't find in a tool. The client fills it in before the kickoff call.

The questions that matter most:

  • Three to five direct competitors.
  • The KPIs they care about and how they track them now.
  • Priority locations, if they have more than one.
  • How they collect reviews today.
  • Planned changes, like a migration, redesign or new location.
  • The services they want more of, and why customers pick them over competitors.

The competitor list feeds the keyword gap analysis (#6). The planned changes stop the agency from optimizing a site that's about to be replaced. The full question list is in the SEO client onboarding questionnaire template.

#8: Discovery Call Template

The discovery call template keeps sales calls consistent. It has the prospect's details at the top, prep notes, rapport questions and the questions that qualify the deal.

Fill it in 15 minutes before the call. Run the site through a crawler and Ahrefs or Semrush, note one to three issues, and read the prospect's intake form. Then record the call, so the notes don't depend on memory.

The questions themselves are in discovery call questions for agencies.

#7: GA4 Audit Template

The GA4 audit template checks that tracking works before any SEO work starts. If conversions aren't tracked, there's nothing to report in month three.

Each issue gets a status, a priority, a bucket (GA4 or Google Tag Manager) and an owner. The owner column matters: some fixes need the client's developer.

Checks worth including:

  • Data retention. GA4 keeps event-level data for 2 months by default. Set it to 14 months on day one, or year-over-year comparisons in Explorations break.
  • Key events. Calls, forms and bookings marked as key events.
  • Query parameters. Parameters that split one page into many rows.
  • Content groups. GA4 supports a content group dimension, so traffic can be reported by page type instead of page by page.
  • Channel grouping. AI assistants and local listings sorted into the right channels.

#6: Keyword Gap Analysis Template

The keyword gap template finds keywords the client's competitors rank for and the client doesn't. It's usually the fastest source of new page ideas.

The steps are written into the sheet:

  1. Pick five direct competitors from the onboarding questionnaire and the organic competitor reports in Ahrefs or Semrush.
  2. Export each competitor's organic keywords into its own tab.
  3. Merge them in the MASTER tab with volume, difficulty and the client's current position.
  4. Summarize the gaps by topic and map each one to a page.

Ahrefs runs the same comparison inside its Content Gap tool. The sheet still earns its place: it's where the gaps get filtered and turned into a list of pages. More on picking competitors in SEO competitive analysis and on judging keywords in how to do keyword research.

#5: Content Brief Template

The content brief template tells a writer exactly what to write. It's the difference between a page that ranks and a page that needs rewriting.

The fields: topic, primary and secondary keywords, recommended word count, page type, title, H1 and slug. The doc tabs hold the writer and editor instructions, AI prompts, the page outline and the finished page, so the whole page lives in one file.

Build the brief around what the reader needs, not a word count. Google's guidance on helpful, people-first content is a good checklist for the editor. The full field list is in the content brief template guide.

#4: Monthly SEO Report Template

The monthly report template shows the client what changed and what's next. It keeps the client paying, but it doesn't change rankings, so it sits at #4.

A good report template has the same sections every month:

  • Organic traffic and conversions from GA4.
  • Clicks, impressions and position from Search Console. Google explains how it counts impressions, position and clicks.
  • Keyword rankings and, for local clients, map pack grids.
  • Calls and leads, tied back to the pages that drove them.
  • Work completed and work planned for next month.

What to put in each section is in what to include in a monthly SEO report.

#3: Website Quality Audit Template

The website quality audit template reviews every page on the site and gives each one an action. Keep, improve, merge, redirect or remove.

Each row pulls in the URL, page type, main keyword, impressions, position, status code and canonical. The technical checks come from the basics in Google's SEO Starter Guide: crawlable pages, one URL per page, clear titles and descriptive content.

This sits at #3 because it decides most of the work for the first six months. Run it once at the start, then again each year. The full process is in how to run a website quality audit.

#2: SEO Project Plan Template

The project plan template turns the audits into a month-by-month plan. It's what the client signs off on and what the team works from.

Rows are workstreams: analytics, technical, local SEO, content, links and AI visibility. Columns are months. Month one is audits and setup. After that it's implementation, then recurring work.

Two rules make the plan work:

  • Every task traces back to an audit finding. If it can't, it doesn't go in.
  • The fastest wins go first. Profile fixes and easy keywords before the hard terms.

The plan is #2 because a client who sees a clear plan stays. A client who sees a list of random tasks starts asking what they're paying for.

#1: SEO Proposal Template

The SEO proposal template is the most important template an agency has. It wins the client. Without it, the other nine templates never get used.

A proposal template that closes covers:

  1. Where the prospect stands now: traffic, rankings and map pack visibility.
  2. Who's beating them and why.
  3. The keyword and page opportunity, with search volume.
  4. The technical and AI visibility problems.
  5. A phased plan, fastest results first.
  6. A forecast and the price.

Every slide uses the prospect's own data, which is why a proposal template is a structure, not a finished deck. See the SEO proposal template for each section, and how to build an SEO proposal that closes for the sales side.

How to Build an SEO Template Library

Keep every template in one shared folder, named the same way, and copy it for each client. Never edit the master.

A few rules keep the library useful:

  • One prefix for every master file, like "TEMPLATE //", so a Drive search finds them all.
  • Instructions inside the template, like the Getting Started tab in the keyword gap sheet. Nobody reads a separate SOP.
  • One owner per template who updates it when Google or the tools change.
  • Review each template once a year. A GA4 audit written in 2022 won't check AI referral traffic.

Start with the templates for work the agency repeats most. For most SEO agencies, that's reports and content briefs. Then build up to the audit, plan and proposal.

SEO Templates FAQs

What SEO templates does an agency need?

Ten cover the client lifecycle: a discovery call template, onboarding questionnaire, kickoff meeting agenda, GA4 audit, website quality audit, keyword gap analysis, content brief, project plan, monthly report and proposal.

What is the most important SEO template?

The SEO proposal template. It wins the client, and every other template depends on having one.

Should SEO templates be in Google Docs or Sheets?

Use Docs for anything a person reads or fills in, like briefs, questionnaires and call notes. Use Sheets for anything with rows of data, like audits, keyword gaps and project plans. Use slides for proposals.

What should an SEO audit template include?

Every URL on the site with its page type, main keyword, impressions, position, status code and canonical, plus an action for each page: keep, improve, merge, redirect or remove.

How often should SEO templates be updated?

Review each one at least once a year, and right away when Google or a core tool changes how something works.
