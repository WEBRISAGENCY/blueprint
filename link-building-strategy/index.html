Link Building Strategy: The Framework We Use for Clients

Ryan Stewart
Founder, The Blueprint Training
Published: Sep 13, 2021
Updated: Nov 10, 2026
Read: 11 min

Most link building fails before the first email goes out.

The agency builds links. Just not to the pages that make the client money.

A link building strategy decides which pages need links, how many, and from where, before anyone starts outreach.

What is a good link building strategy? Audit the links the site already has, benchmark against the sites that rank for the client's money keywords, and pick the pages that need links. Then earn links and brand mentions from relevant sites through digital PR, local sources and reclaimed links. Measure the result by what the target pages do in Search Console, not by a link count.

Here's the framework we use for client campaigns at WEBRIS, and how we track it in Agency OS.

Here's the short version:

  • Audit fast. 30 to 60 minutes. You're looking for real problems, not every bad link.
  • Benchmark against who ranks. The gap in referring domains tells you the pace and the timeline.
  • Links go to money pages. Service and location pages first, not the blog.
  • Earn them. Google treats paid links that pass ranking credit as link spam.
  • Mentions count now. AI search leans on brand mentions more than raw link counts.
  • Measure the page, not the link. Clicks and impressions on the target page are the scoreboard.

What Is a Link Building Strategy?

A link building strategy is a plan for which pages on a site need backlinks, how many, from which types of sites, and at what pace, based on what the sites that already rank have. It's the document that turns "we'll build links" into a monthly target the client can check.

It has three parts:

  • Where you are. The link profile audit.
  • Where you need to be. The competitive benchmark.
  • How you get there. The target pages, link types and monthly pace.

It's one of the month-one deliverables in our list of 31 SEO deliverables.

Do Links Still Matter in 2026?

Yes, but they're no longer the only off-site signal that matters. Links still help pages rank in Google. AI search answers lean harder on how often a brand gets mentioned around the web.

An Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands (May 2025) measured which factors lined up with how often a brand appeared in Google's AI Overviews. Branded web mentions had the strongest correlation. Raw backlink counts had one of the weakest.

Correlation isn't cause. But read that again: getting talked about beat piling up links by 3 to 1.

So the strategy below builds both: links to the pages that need to rank, and mentions that make the brand show up in AI answers. More on that in why your agency needs to sell blended search.

Step 1: Audit the Links the Site Already Has

The link audit records what the site has before you start and catches the rare problem that needs fixing. Spend 30 to 60 minutes on it. If nothing jumps out, move on.

Pull the backlink profile from Ahrefs or Semrush and check six things:

  • Anchor text. Exact-match keyword anchors dominating the profile is a red flag.
  • Spammy anchors. Adult, gambling or pharma patterns.
  • Country domains. A sudden spike of links from unrelated foreign domains.
  • Velocity. Referring domains over time. A spike from press coverage is good. A spike from syndicated junk isn't.
  • Low-quality links. Sort by lowest rating, then check the sites by hand. A small site isn't automatically a bad one.
  • Negative SEO. Put it together. Is this an attack, or the leftovers of a previous agency?

Don't reach for the disavow file by default. Google's own guidance says "most sites will not need to use this tool." Use it only when there are a lot of spammy, artificial links and they've caused, or are likely to cause, a manual action.

I'll be honest: Google ignores most junk links on its own. The audit is mainly your baseline, so you can show the client what changed.

Step 2: Benchmark Against the Sites That Rank

The competitive benchmark tells you how far behind the site is, how fast you need to build, and where the best links are coming from. This is where the strategy actually comes from.

Pull the two or three sites that rank for the client's money keywords. Not the competitors the client names. The ones Google shows.

Compare four things:

  • Referring domains. The size of the gap.
  • Velocity. How many new referring domains they add a month. That's the minimum pace you need to beat.
  • Anchor mix. What a natural profile looks like for sites that already rank.
  • Link sources. Their best referring domains. Many of these will link to your client too, if you give them a reason.

This also manages expectations. When the top competitor has 500 referring domains and the client has 30, nobody expects page one in 60 days.

One warning. Treat tool numbers as directional. Authority scores and traffic estimates are guesses, and lately they're often wrong. Compare sites against each other, not against a number.

The full process is in how to run an SEO competitive analysis.

Step 3: Pick the Pages That Need Links

Links go to the pages that rank for searches that make the client money, and that are close enough to page one that links will move them. For most local businesses, that's service pages and location pages.

A blog post that ranks for a question nobody buys from doesn't need links. A practice area page sitting at position 8 for "car accident lawyer" in their city does.

For each target page, write down:

  • The one keyword it targets.
  • Where it ranks today.
  • How many referring domains the pages above it have.
  • How many links it needs per month to close the gap.

If you don't have a keyword for every page yet, start with keyword research. The website quality audit tells you which pages are worth keeping in the first place.

Step 4: Choose the Link Types You'll Build

A balanced campaign uses three layers: citations as the foundation, earned links from relevant sites as the core, and digital PR for authority and mentions. The mix depends on the gap you found in step 2.

Before you buy anything, know the rule. Google's spam policies (last updated August 2026) call it link spam to buy or sell links for ranking purposes, including paid guest posts with keyword-rich anchors. Paid links are fine when they're marked rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow", but then they don't pass ranking credit.

Read that again. If you pay for a placement that passes ranking credit, you're taking a risk on your client's behalf. Tell them.

The links that hold up are the ones you earn: a local news story, a sponsorship, a resource page, a quote in an industry publication.

Step 5: Start With the Links You Already Earned

The fastest links are the ones the site already has but isn't getting credit for. Do these in month one.

Broken link reclamation. Filter the backlink profile for links pointing to pages that return a 404. Redirect each dead URL to the closest live page, and the value from those links flows again.

Unlinked brand mentions. Search for the client's name on sites that mention them without a link. Ask each site to add one. They already know the brand, so it's the easiest ask in link building.

Existing relationships. Vendors, associations, chambers of commerce, sponsorships the client already pays for. Most of these will add a link if someone asks.

Step 6: Build Mentions for AI Search

To show up in AI answers, the brand needs to be talked about on the sites AI platforms trust, with or without a link. That's the gap the Ahrefs data points to.

The same work that earns good links earns mentions:

  • Digital PR. Quotes and expert commentary in local news and industry publications.
  • Reviews. A steady flow on Google and the platforms buyers in that niche use.
  • Community. Real participation where the audience asks questions, like Reddit.
  • Consistency. The same name, address and description everywhere the brand appears.

At WEBRIS, a dedicated Digital PR Strategist runs link acquisition and citation building for our law firm clients. The PR coverage does double duty: links for Google, mentions for AI.

Step 7: Measure the Page, Not the Link

The way to judge a link building campaign is what happens to the target pages: clicks, impressions and rankings in Search Console, and the leads those pages bring in. Not how many links you built.

Twenty links to a page that doesn't move is a failed month. Three links to a page that climbs into the top three is a great one.

How to Present a Link Building Strategy to Clients

Present the strategy as four answers: where your links stand today, where you stand against the sites beating you, what we'll build each month, and when to expect results.

Clients push back on link building more than any other SEO service, because they can't see the work happening. The strategy fixes that. The gap is on a slide, the plan is a number, and the report shows the pages moving.

Set the timeline honestly. Links usually take three to six months to show up in rankings, longer when the gap is big.

Link Building Mistakes

  • ❌ Building links to the blog. The money pages need them more.
  • ❌ Reporting link counts. Report what the target pages did.
  • ❌ Exact-match anchors everywhere. It's the pattern Google's spam policies call out.
  • ❌ Buying links without telling the client the risk. It's their site.
  • ❌ Disavowing by default. Google says most sites don't need it.
  • ❌ Trusting tool scores as truth. Use them to compare, not to forecast.
  • ✅ Earned links and mentions, aimed at money pages, measured by page performance.

Link Building Strategy FAQs

How many backlinks do I need per month?

Enough to beat the pace of the sites that rank above you. Check how many new referring domains they add each month and build faster than that, starting with the pages closest to page one.

How long does link building take to work?

Usually three to six months before rankings move, and longer when the site is far behind its competitors on referring domains.

Is buying backlinks against Google's rules?

Buying links that pass ranking credit is link spam under Google's spam policies. Paid links are allowed when they're marked rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow", but those don't pass ranking credit.

Do backlinks help with AI search?

Less than brand mentions do. In an Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands, branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664, compared with 0.218 for the number of backlinks.

Should I disavow bad backlinks?

Usually not. Google says most sites don't need the disavow tool. Use it when a large number of spammy links have caused, or are likely to cause, a manual action.
